# General rule of thumb: beginner lifters should use presses for the majority of chest exercises

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/6QAujh8S2nM>

I said that a fly can be just as effective as a press because it's essentially the same motion that you're doing. If you're doing say a pec deck fly, same motion as doing a flat press that's being loaded. This guy says you can progressively overload presses more though without being as risky to the shoulder joint. Why? It's the same motion. The shoulder joint is being loaded in the same way. All that comes down to is load management and making sure you're not loading more than you're capable of. You know, I was just thinking and maybe what he's trying to say but he's just mixing the terms is that you can load more weight with a press. It's not progressive overload. You can load more weight with a press compared to a fly because it's going to be a shorter moment arm. Your elbow is bent. So, you're going to be able to load more weight. Maybe that's what he's saying. Because if you were to try and load the exact same amount of weight that you're able to press as you would on a fly for the same number of reps, yeah, you'll probably snap your shoulder off. Here's my general rule of thumb. If you're a beginner lifter, majority of your chest exercises should be presses because you're still going to greatly benefit from that being a compound movement. So, you're still going to see good growth from not only your pecs, but your triceps, the medial and lateral head, the anterior delts as well. So, maximize the gains from that as much as you can while you're still new to lifting and then worry about isolating later. Once you're more intermediate advanced, then you can look at doing more flies than presses because maybe you've already got really good arms and shoulders and you want to do a better job of biasing the pecs. That's where that would come into play. In summary, flies can be just as effective as a press.
