This right here is the lifting loop of hell. And so many lifters stop making progress because they get trapped in this loop and they don't even know that they're in it. So, here are the three fundamental steps to getting trapped in this loop and how you can avoid them so that you can make progress long term. Step number one is to do some kind of exercise that gives you pain or discomfort or clunkiness. Step number two, instead of assuming that the exercise is the problem, you assume that you and your body are broken and that your body is the problem. And so step number three, as a consequence of that, you go off and do some kind of non-specific exercise that you found on the internet that is supposed to correct the issue. So let's give examples here. Imagine you were doing a pull down and you have some kind of pain in your shoulder. A lot of times people don't assume that the pull down is the problem and the way they're executing the pull down is the problem. They assume their shoulder is [ __ ] up in some intrinsic way. So what they go and do is they go and do some kind of non-specific rotator cuff exercise, whatever crazy exercise they found on Instagram or Tik Tok last night, and they do that exercise. And the problem about doing that exercise a lot of times it gives people temporary pain relief. Novelty is a really great thing for pain relief. So they go back to the original exercise without having changed anything whatsoever and they do the pull down again and it feels a little bit better. The problem with this however is that when you return to the original exercise next time when you try to add more load, do more reps, progress the actual stimula so that you can continue to grow and progress, it ends up feeling like crap again because you never actually change anything about the exercise to begin with. you just did something else to band-aid the issue so that you could continue doing the exercise. So the solution to not getting trapped in this loop is to address the problem on the level at which it occurs. And instead of assuming that your body is the problem, assume that the way that you're doing the exercise is. So if I'm doing a pull down, I'm not going to assume that if my shoulder hurts that my shoulder is the problem. I'm going to assume that maybe I need to adjust the angle. Instead of pulling a little bit wider, maybe I'll pull more narrow. Instead of pulling with a neutral grip, maybe I'll pull with a pronated grip or a subnated grip. Instead of maybe pulling at this angle, maybe I'll pull more forward to backward. Maybe that will feel better. Maybe instead of using this much range of motion, I'll stop the range of motion a little bit shy if the top portion of the range is the only thing that hurts. The point is that there are so many different exercise variables that you can adjust that you should never fall into the trap of assuming that you have to do an exercise one single way. Because if you fall into this trap, you're going to spend half of your session warming up for an exercise with non-specific activities. you're going to be wasting your time in the gym and you're going to spend half your session doing things that aren't actually producing results.
