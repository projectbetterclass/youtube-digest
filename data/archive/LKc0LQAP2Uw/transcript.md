# Cats are like machines

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/LKc0LQAP2Uw>

Confession, I used to really hate cats and I used to think dogs were just a superior animal. And this perfectly mirrored my hate toward machines and my love for free weights. About a decade ago, I I loved free weights for the same reasons that I loved dogs. All right, they're predictable, they're familiar, and they follow you around wherever you move, right? And I hated machines for the same reason I hated cats. They're kind of unfamiliar, they seem somewhat dangerous, and they always seem to have their own agenda. But when my wife said to me, "Ben, we're not going to move in together unless we get two cats." It took me all of about an hour to see just how ignorant I actually was. Because cats, much like machines in the gym, have a fundamentally different beauty about them. They're misunderstood and then they're blamed for our poor understanding of how to relate to them. They have their own agenda, yeah, but that's exactly what makes them so incredible to be around. And when you go to other countries or other gyms, and you see that new cat for the first time, you see that new machine you've never met before, a combination of this familiar and unfamiliar thing meets you in a way that's just so exciting. And, you know, will I need to adjust the machine 100 times or or will a couple of reps just give me this buttered up experience of a lovely resistance profile, right? Will this new cat I've never met, uh, that's approaching me bite me and send me to the hospital or or will it become a traveling companion that I'll never forget? Right, to understand the cat and to understand the machine is to respect their intricate design. And to assume that we don't know how it will respond to us and how we might need to change our approach to allow for a good relationship. Now, I've come to love cats and machines for precisely the same reasons that I used to hate them. They don't always respond the way I expect them to. They require me to pay attention, to experiment, and to sometimes totally reconsider what I thought that I knew. And I found that the more willing I am to meet them on their own terms, the more that there is to appreciate.
