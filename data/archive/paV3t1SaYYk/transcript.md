# Old video but I think the message is helpful for those in my DMs asking me about rehab

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/paV3t1SaYYk>

If you exploded your pec tendon doing barbell bench, would you do barbell bench again? >> I just felt something not good. >> For a lot of people, the answer would probably be no, but I have a lot of faith in my rehab and my body's ability to recover. This was my first time barbell benching since my pec tear of October last year, but I have been doing dumbbell bench regularly about twice a week. And I'm happy to say that my strength is back to normal now, especially when you factor in that I'm around 30 lbs later than I was pre-tear. But considering that it was my first time doing barbell bench again in so long, I decided to take it slow and just get a feel for the exercise again. It definitely felt a bit humbling going from repping out 315 to having 185 on the bar and this being a somewhat difficult set. But just like I tell my clients, never let your ego get ahead of you when you're picking your working weight for your sets. I promise, unless you're Russ or he [music] nobody around you is paying attention to how much you lift and nobody cares when you tell them how much you lift. Pick a weight that you can maintain the same technique with, then once you've earned the right to increase the load by maxing out your rep range or perhaps even getting a rep or two above that, then increase by 3 to 5%. Anyways, I apologize for traumatizing some of you guys with my pec tear. But hopefully I've served as an example that if you're consistent, persistent, and believe in your body, then you can come back even stronger.
