# "Front squats are better for quads"

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LhTCRImOHes>

Front squats are better for training quads, right? Not so fast. Compare these two squatting variations, a back squat and a front squat. If you just compared these two, you'd say, "Okay, the back squat is definitely more hip dominant than the front squat." because the front squat is a little bit more upright and maybe there's even a little bit more loading on the quads because of where the bar goes. The problem with front squats though is that they're loaded on the front side of your body. And it's really freaking easy just to kind of tip the bar forward and this puts a lot of demand on your upper back and your spinal muscles to be able to just hold you upright. When the bar is on your back, it's really easy to hold there. So, I often find that when people do front squats, the rate limiting factor is often not load on the quads, but actually challenge in the spine and challenge not to dump the bar forward. If you make this one small change to your back squat, just elevating your heels with a plate or a wedge or anything similar, you can basically just do everything that you were trying to do with the front squat and targeting more of the quads. Now, you're doing so in a way that's really, really stable. The bar is not falling forward, you can stay super upright and your knees are basically doing all the traveling forward to backward as you descend directly downward. This doesn't make front squats bad by any means and they can be a great exercise if you want to integrate a challenge at your trunk, your quads and your hips. But, for maximal hypertrophy stimulus to the quads, I highly, highly recommend the heel elevated back squat over any kind of front squat.
