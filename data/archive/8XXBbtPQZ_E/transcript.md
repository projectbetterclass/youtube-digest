# How does your list line up with mine?

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/8XXBbtPQZ_E>

These guys had some interesting takes that I didn't really agree with, so I will give you my take on these exercises and where I would place them in this tier list. Standing cable fly I like because you can progressively overload it. However, the bracing is going to be an issue for a lot of people, especially as they get stronger. If you were setting this up seated, then I would rate this higher, but I would personally put this around that B to C tier. If this were a seated cable fly, then that could very well push it up to the A or S tier since you now have very good bracing. Push-ups are okay. If you don't have anything to progressively overload with, a backpack to add some extra weight, then you're going to hit a wall with those pretty quickly for the average person who is in relatively good shape. So, me personally, I'm going to put those around the E tier. I can see somebody making the argument that it is one of the easiest ways to start training your chest with no equipment, so that could bump it up. Dumbbell pec fly, I would put that pretty similarly to the standing cable fly. However, since there is going to be better bracing, I'd probably bump that up one. So, I would put that comfortably in the A to B tier. And it looks like another exercise that we don't quite agree on. I do really like dumbbell work for pressing because I find it very easy on people's joints. You can adjust the arm path, and it can be done in pretty much any commercial gym, so plenty of reasons to do it. As somebody gets really strong, that's where I think it's beneficial to switch out the dumbbell work for more machine work, and this is an exercise that I would comfortably place in the S tier. A machine press is going to be one of your best bang for your buck exercises for the chest. It's very stable, and it's something that you can continue progressively overloading considering that it has very good bracing. Not sure what that exercise did to them to deserve the C tier, but I I would place that much higher. Dumbbell decline bench press, I don't think this is something that most people really need to be including within their program considering that the low pecs get hit very well even in a flat press, and there are exercises that I like a bit more for biasing the lower chest. So, I would personally put that around the D to E tier. Incline barbell press, I would really have around that A to B tier. I think the difference between that A and B tier would depend on how people's elbows, their joints tolerate barbell pressing compared to something like dumbbell or machine pressing. But yeah, A to B tier for me. Barbell pressing is going to be more stable than dumbbell pressing, but I do really like dumbbells because of how easy it is on my joints. And flat barbell press I would put in the same place around that A to B tier. Machine pressing and pec deck flies should definitely be in S tier though.
