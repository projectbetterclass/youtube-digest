# Pullups Suck For Hypertrophy?

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/qm_u5YGEJlk>

I swear to God, man, if I hear one more science-based lifter say that pull-ups are a bad exercise because they're unstable, I'm gonna do something, okay? Because they're not. I mean, first off, in a pull down, the bar is moving freely, whereas in a pull-up, it's fixed, meaning that part of the lift is actually more stable, not less. Yeah, your legs aren't locked in place like they are on a pull down machine, but they also don't need to be since gravity is already acting as the anchor by pulling your body straight toward the floor. Now, sure, that does make it easier to sway a little bit, but if you just train like an adult and perform the lift under control with a slight pause and deliberate negative, then that won't be an issue. And as you get stronger and continue to add weight, you'll find that the extra load naturally locks you into place even more. Now, let's be clear. I'm not saying you have to do pull-ups to maximize back gains. I'm not even saying they're better than pull downs. I'm just saying that if you truly think pull-ups are a poor choice for hypertrophy, it's probably because you've never actually done them over a consistent period. And like a lot of social media lifting experts nowadays, you're relying way too much on theory and not nearly enough on real world experience.
