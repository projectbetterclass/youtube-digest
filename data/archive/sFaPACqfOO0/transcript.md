# STOP doing this during hip thrusts!

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/sFaPACqfOO0>

So, I totally disagree with this. Let me explain why. The idea is that thrusting like this with a kind of scoop in your hips takes tension off of the low back. But this kind of pelvis scooping method is actually the only way to guarantee that the movement is driven through your lower back and your spine. This is an action called posterior pelvic tilting, which in this context is actually controlled by both the abs and the erectors. So in this context, the glute max is not going to be the thing to tuck your pelvis underneath of you. And what I would advise is keeping your entire spine in that relatively neutral straighter position as you just drive your leg downward directly into the floor. You want to imagine that your belly button and your sternum are pointed in the same direction roughly speaking the entire time. This method will keep tension off of your low back and will place more of it proportionally onto your glutes because more of the range of motion in this case is occurring through the hip joint. Now, is the tucking method bad? I certainly don't think so. From a pure physics perspective, the tucking method will involve more of the spine muscles because the spine is moving more. Whereas the braced version, where your entire body is tilting upward and downward, involve less of that stuff and more of just pure glutes. If you're feeling your low back a lot in this exercise, trying to perform it the neutral way, what I would advise is spending more time pausing at the top position, lowering the weight, and then working back up with that technique so you can be more specific to the glutes and less specific to the spine muscles.
