# The Secret To Targeting Your Lats

Most people that can't feel, target, or grow their lats don't understand this one key aspect of their function, which is that the primary function of lats isn't actually to pull the arm downward or inward toward the body. The function they're even better at is known as scapular depression, where the entire shoulder girdle, the shoulder blade, the collar bone, and the upper arm are all getting pulled downward like that. So, what does this actually mean in practice? Well, it means that whenever you're trying to target your lats, instead of just thinking about pulling with your hand or even your elbow, you want to think about pulling primarily with your armpit and allowing your elbow to follow through. My favorite way of getting people to learn how to do this is with a single arm pull down like this. Instead of starting with normal reps, what you want to do is aim to pull your armpit down toward your same side pocket. You also want to make sure that the cable isn't directly overhead, but rather slightly in front of you to give you room to actually pull back and down simultaneously. Once you feel like you have good control of that depression motion, which again will only really be performed by the lats pulling downward like this, then you can integrate the motion into a full pulling exercise where you're allowing everything to move together. So you need to make sure that you're actually allowing the shoulder blade to move up on the eccentric and back down toward the ground on the concentric. And once you learn how to use that skill of actually moving your armpit first, you'll pretty much be able to feel your lats on any exercise that you use. And instead of feeling your forearms and your arms, you'll just feel everything right in the belly of the lats.
