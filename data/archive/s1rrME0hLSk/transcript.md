# How to fix wrist pain when pressing

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/s1rrME0hLSk>

Why do you get wrist pain when you press, and how can you fix it? Whenever you're pressing, you're using something that's directed backward through the forearm, and so you can visualize the line of force of whatever implement you're holding going like this. Now, if you're holding something in the base of your palm, notice how the line of force goes directly through the wrist. But, if you grip something in your fingertips, notice the distance now that the line of force moves away from the wrist. When the implement you're using shifts upward in the palm, that means you have to start to create a flexion against the load instead of it just being stacked nice and stable on top of your wrist. So, to fix this, regardless of the implement that you're using, make sure that the handle is as deep as possible on your palm, and that the implement isn't trying to bend your wrist backwards at all. And a very simple strategy to use to actually make sure you're doing it correctly is to see if you can actually hold the implement without wrapping your fingers around it first. Because if you can hold the implement and actually push into it, it means the load is directly over the wrist. But, if you can't actually hold the implement without the use of your fingers, then you know it's too high in the palm.
