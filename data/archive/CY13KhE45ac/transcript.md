# You don't need to go to failure with every single set

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/CY13KhE45ac>

Some people are just blessed with nutty tricep shape. It gives them these huge arms even as a natural lifter. I mean, this is very impressive. He's got some serious weight on these push downs, but I would say that going to failure three times and then drop setting really not necessary. If you're going from that much weight at the start to this much weight and then he drops it even further, you can't really think that you're getting the same stimulus from this as you were that first set. At that point you're just fried and you're no longer recruiting those high threshold motor units that control those big muscle fibers that seek growth whenever you train. You're essentially just be stimulating muscle fibers that have already reached their full potential growth wise. If you're doing multiple sets then I'm just not a fan of doing every set to failure because then you're just not getting as much out of the sets that come later into the workout. You would see much better growth and get more out of these sets if you just stated around one to three reps in reserve for those earlier sets. Considering that we're just training elbow flexion and extension here, I would also say that there's a lot of sets here, especially considering that they're all to failure. All of the triceps were already trained with that push down, they were trained with that overhead extension, and then you also have dips in there as well. And this would be completely redundant considering there's a preacher curl and a hammer curl.
