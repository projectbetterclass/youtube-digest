1. Amazon
1. Current Cashflow: The company does not return cash to 
stockholders, either as dividends or stock buybacks. In the 
most recent year, the company had about $1 billion in FCFE.
2. Cash Balance: The company has accumulated almost $35 
billion in cash, about 5% of firm value.
3. Project Quality: It generates  positive excess returns 
(approximately 4%) and has delivered significant excess 
returns to its stockholders. It has significant growth 
opportunities in multiple businesses.
4. Leverage: The company is at about its optimal debt ratio.
Bottom line: Amazon should not return cash.
6
2. Alphabet
1. Current Cashflow: The company does not return cash to 
stockholders, either as dividends or stock buybacks. In the 
most recent year, the company had about $11 billion in 
FCFE.
2. Cash Balance: The company has accumulated almost $102 
billion in cash, about 14% of firm value.
3. Project Quality: It generates  positive excess returns 
(approximately 14%) but almost all of it is from its search 
engine business. The rest of its businesses are money losers.
4. Leverage: The company is under levered, with a debt ratio 
of 2% and an optimal of 30.00%.
Bottom line: Alphabet should start returning cash to its 
stockholders
7
3. Apple
1. Current Cashflow: The company returned more cash than 
any other company in the world ($31 billion) but its FCFE 
was even higher (almost $60 billion)
2. Cash Balance: The company has accumulated almost $250 
billion in cash, about 28% of firm value.
3. Project Quality: It generates  positive excess returns 
(approximately 19%) but much of it comes from its smart 
phone business, which does not require massive 
reinvestment.
4. Leverage: The company is under levered, with a debt ratio 
of about 12% and an optimal debt ratio of 40%
Bottom line: Apple is doling out more cash than any company 
in history, but it should accelerate its cash return even more.
8
4.  Coca Cola
1. Current Cashflow: The company returned $9.3 billion in 
cash but its FCFE was even higher (almost $12 billion)
2. Cash Balance: The company has accumulated almost 
$20 billion in cash, about 10% of firm value.
3. Project Quality: It generates  positive excess returns 
(approximately 12%) but its core beverage business is 
stagnant and growth is scarce.
4. Leverage: The company is under levered, with a debt 
ratio of about 18.5% and an optimal debt ratio of 30%
Bottom line: Coca Cola has been paying large dividends, 
but will have to increase cash return (perhaps with 
buybacks).
9
5. Pacific Gas & Electric 
1. Current Cashflow: The company returned $784 million 
in cash, higher than its FCFE of $564 million.
2. Cash Balance: The company has accumulated almost 
$450 million in cash, less than 1% of firm value.
3. Project Quality: It generates  positive excess returns 
(approximately 1.25%) but it is a regulated company. It 
does have investment opportunities in green energy.
4. Leverage: The company is over levered, with a debt 
ratio of about 36% and an optimal debt ratio of 20%
Bottom line: PG&E should scale back its dividends, pay 
down debt and redirect some cash to green energy 
investments.
10
6. Panera Bread
1. Current Cashflow: The company returned $982 million 
in cash, higher than its FCFE of $806 million.
2. Cash Balance: The company has accumulated almost 
$153 million in cash, about 3% of firm value.
3. Project Quality: It generates  negative excess returns 
(approximately -1.03%) and operates in a saturated, 
highly competitive business.
4. Leverage: The company is under levered, with a debt 
ratio of about 23% and an optimal debt ratio of 30%
Bottom line: Panera should continue to pay out more 
than its FCFE as dividends, until its debt ratio converges 
on its optimal.
