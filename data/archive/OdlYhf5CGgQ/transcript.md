# Progressive Overload is a LIE

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/OdlYhf5CGgQ>

Progressive overload is a complete lie. If we could all add five pounds to every single lift, every single workout, we'd all be able to lift like 1,000 lbs. Once you're past like the complete newbie phase, like congratulations. You were able to add 5 lbs when your squat was like 10 lb. So, he's absolutely right. You can't be expecting to add weight to the bar every single week. But that's also not what progressive overload actually is. Progressive overload just means an increase in performance over time, and it doesn't always have to be in the form of additional weight. It can also mean more reps with the same weight. One extra rep is still significant, even half a rep counts. I personally log third and quarter reps on some lifts because as you get more advanced, it's normal for progress to happen in smaller increments. It could even mean the same weight in reps. But if last week the final one was an all-out grinder and this week it moved faster, that's still progression. And if you are adding weight, it doesn't always have to be 5 lb either. It could be 2 1/2 lb. It could even be one pound as long as you're consistently moving forward in some measurable way. And if your workouts, nutrition, and recovery are on point, progress won't magically come to a halt unless you're literally at the upper limit of your genetic potential.
