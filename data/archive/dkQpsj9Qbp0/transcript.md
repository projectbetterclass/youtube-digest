The seated calf raise is the best way to train [music] the sole. Most people do this wrong. This is how you do it right. So step number one is you want to push this down as far as you possibly can. A lot of people try to make it really comfortable by starting the movement like this. The problem is when you start it, you won't get enough of a stretch when you do finally perform. So my advice would be to take as low as you can possibly take it. Wedge yourself in there so [music] it can no longer go any lower. From this point, simply press up onto the balls of your feet. Move the safety way. Hold on to it and let yourself sink [music] all the way down. When you feel a deep stretch in your hamstrings, simply just push back up onto your toes. I like to have my body weight forward so I can sit on the pressure of my calves. All the way [music] down, stretch, and then all the way up. If you start this too high, you won't be able to go all the way down before you hit the stop. So, start as [music] low as you can. Bonus tip. With this movement, you can perform it until it no longer comes up. When it no longer comes off, you simply just wrigle [music] your feet out of it and you've completed the movement.
