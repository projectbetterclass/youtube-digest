# How I deal with time off training

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/dAv2MtNkxCA>

Week off from lifting and not sure how to approach your first session? Well, here's exactly what I did. Despite what a lot of people tell you, you will lose a little bit of muscle in a week off of lifting. But, unless you're bedridden for that week, it really shouldn't be so much that you should lose a significant amount of strength. That's why when I take some time off of the gym, I'm just looking to match my previous PRs, which is what I did this session. And if I do happen to lose a rep or two using the same amount of load from the last session, it's really not a big deal to me. I try not to be too hard on myself because I know I'll likely hit the same amount of reps in the next session. Now, if I took a bit longer off and I know that I'm feeling a bit weaker, looking a bit smaller, then what I'll do instead of trying to match the same number of reps with the same amount of weight that I used in the previous session, knowing full well that I won't be able to do so, I'll lower the weight by 2 to 5%. From there, I will try to match the same number of reps from the previous session with that lower load. Then from there, I should be able to increase the amount of weight that I'm using each session as muscle memory is a thing and I should gain it back relatively quickly. Now, if you wanted to avoid this altogether and you're not able to lift as much for a week, it's ideal if you can hit at least one to two full body sessions a week to help with maintaining your muscle mass. And it's not like you have to do a ton of volume on each of those days. You could be doing one to two sets for each exercise and still maintain mass.
