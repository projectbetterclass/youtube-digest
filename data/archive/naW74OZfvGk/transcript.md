Here are five suboptimal exercises that so-called science-based lifters are always talking smack about, but that are perfectly fine choices for building muscle. Dumbbell lateral raises. Oh, but there's no tension at the bottom. Yeah, but there's tension in the middle and at the top, which also stimulates growth. Train these close to failure with solid form, and your side delts will get smashed. Basic ass free curls. If you want to do this, this or this, then that's all good. But ultimately, the function of your biceps is to bend your elbow. It's really not that deep. And even if one curling variation is superior to another, the difference is probably very minor. Triceps push downs with a regular straight bar. If a different attachment feels more comfortable on your wrists and elbows, then go for it. But if a straight bar feels fine, then it's fine. I enjoy a good 45° crossbody cuffed variation as much as the next guy. But you don't have to do that to build big triceps. Pull-ups. According to some people, these are no longer a good choice since they're quote unquote unstable, right? because no one has ever built an impressive back doing weighted pull-ups. Perform them under control, steadily add weight over time, and they're still one of the absolute best upper body exercises there is. Dumbbell flies. Oh, but there's no tension at the top. You mean the same way there's no tension at the top of a dumbbell press either, or an overhead press or a squat or a deadlift? I don't know why dumbbell flies get picked on for that reason. Every exercise will be heavier or lighter at different points in the range. And that's not a bad thing because you don't need constant tension to build muscle in the first place.
