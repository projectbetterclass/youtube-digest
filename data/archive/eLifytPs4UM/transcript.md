# Stop Worrying About "Time Under Tension"

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/eLifytPs4UM>

Check out this video of me doing a barbell curl and see if you can spot what I'm doing wrong. If you can't, you're likely making the same mistake I am here and not just on curls, but maybe even every exercise you're doing. You need a hint? It has to do with the timing of the rep, specifically how quickly I'm doing them. Without a conscious effort to slow down, most sets of 12 curls will take just 24 seconds. Instead, shoot for 48 seconds or 4 seconds per rep. Just make sure that you try and lift that weight up as explosively as possible and then save as much of those 4 seconds for the slow eccentric lowering. >> No, time under tension isn't something you need to worry about. And purposely dragging your sets out over a longer period is not better for growth. If this was actually true, then why do we consistently see that low reps, even as few as 3 to five per set, build just as much muscle as higher reps, and that faster eccentrics, produce the same growth as slower ones? It's because the levels of tension only get high enough to stimulate significant growth on the last few reps of a set taken close to failure. and it occurs on the concentric phase since you're way stronger on the eccentric and so you'll never be challenging the muscle close to its full capacity on that part of the lift. Longer sets feel harder because of greater metabolite buildup, but muscle burn is not an independent driver of growth. Now, yes, you should still control the negative to keep your reps consistent and minimize joint stress. And if you just prefer a slower eccentric, that's fine, but you don't need to time your sets and hit some arbitrary number of total seconds. All you really need to do is train close to failure with good technique for at least four to five reps on most sets and progressively overload over time.
