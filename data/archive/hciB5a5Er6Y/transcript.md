In this video, you're going to learn about how to train all of your abs based on biomechanics and physics and not guesswork. First up, we have the rectus abdominis, aka the six-pack. It attaches from here and runs down to here. And so, what that means is it pulls these two points closer together. As a consequence, its main function is to create rounding of the spine as in a crunching exercise. So in the gym, what we need to do to load this crunching action is basically create a situation where we're spreading these points apart. I.e. we need to use a resistance that is arching our back. An exercise that would function from the top down would be something like a rope crunch where the rope is in your hand or anchored around your shoulders where you're creating a bending against the load that is trying to straighten your back. This could be on the ground or with your butt up against the cable column or even on something like a lat pull down machine. If you wanted to do it from bottom up, what you could do is something more like a hanging leg raise where you're essentially tucking your hips underneath of you to create that spine rounding from the pelvis upward. You could also do something like a reverse guard hammer where you're swinging your legs up and where you're controlling the spine rounding down on the way down. Both of these options are fantastic for the entire six-pack. So, just pick the ones that you like more. Next up, we have the external obliques, which run kind of at a diagonal angle this way, and which run down into the hip like this. You want to target the fibers of the external oblique that run more horizontally. You want to do something that kind of looks like some sort of cable chop where hopefully the rest of your body is anchored down and where you can just create a stable trunk rotation. My favorite way to set this up on a cable station is to use a bench to prop up my hip so that I can just perform the cable rotations that way. To train more of the side portions of this muscle, what you would want to do is some kind of side bending activity. You could do it with a free weight, you could do it with a machine, or you could just do it with something like a cable. And the internal obliques have a very similar function compared to the external, which is that they not only create trunk rotation just like the external obliques, they also create trunk side bending. So to target this muscle, what you want to do is pretty much just the same rotationbased exercise as before. Just recognize that if you are twisting to the left that your right external oblique is going to twist you to the left and your left internal oblique is also going to twist you to the left. And in addition, some kind of side bending motion to train the fibers that are closer to the back side of the hip. So in any ab training program, I would basically focus on three distinct motions for complete development. One that is a kind of crunching or leg raising activity for the rectus abdominis. A second which is some kind of trunk rotation activity to train both the internal and the external obliques. And then some kind of side bending motion to train the portion of the internal and the external obliques that is more on the side of the body as compared to the ones that are running more horizontally.
