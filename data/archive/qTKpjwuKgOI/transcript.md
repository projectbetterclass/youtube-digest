Beta 9 is the first version of Tesla's  vision-only system, meaning the cars will no longer rely on radar to drive themselves.  Let me quickly point out a few things here: Radar stands for radio detection and ranging,  which is exactly what it sounds like. Radars shoot radio waves out, detect the things that  reflect those radio waves back, and estimate the range of the object based on how long it took  those radio waves to return. Range information helps cars do things like know when to brake  for obstacles and maintain the speed of the car in front of them. Because they use radio  waves, radars can see through snow, rain, fog, dust, and have no trouble seeing  at night, unlike normal cameras. However, radars tend to be pretty  low resolution compared to cameras, meaning they have a harder time  telling the shape of an object, so cameras tend to do that job. Radars can also  have a lot of false detections, which can cause issues like phantom braking when the car tries to  stop you from hitting something that isn't really there. So, removing radars means removing the  benefits of all-weather detection and ranging and the drawbacks of trying to clean up, combine,  and make sense of noisy radar data in real time. However, the magic here is that Tesla  already has radar data alongside all of the billions of miles of camera data for  their fleet so far, so they can use that to help train their vision-only version of FSD to  do better depth detection.
