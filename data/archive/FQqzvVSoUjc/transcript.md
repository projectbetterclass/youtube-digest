# Stop training hamstrings

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/FQqzvVSoUjc>

If you don't compete, you don't have to hit hamstrings. I mean, think about it. When you're showing someone your legs, you showed them your quads. You don't show your hamstrings. If you're showing someone your back, you're not pulling down your pants to show your hamstrings and back. You're only doing that if you compete. So, theoretically, if you don't compete, you don't have to hit hamstrings. You only have to worry about your quads. Hell no, dude. I've been saying this for years, but I think aesthetic hamstrings are one of the most underrated muscle groups there is. And the idea that no one notices them also isn't true. Not saying mine are massive or anything, but I actually have been specifically complimented on my hamstrings quite a few times. And not just by men either. Probably 90% men, but who's counting. The point is, having those thick slabs of meat hanging off the back of your legs really does give your lower body that strong, athletic look. Not to mention literally making you more athletic since the hamstrings play a key role in sprinting. So, don't skimp out on hamstring training, not only for the gains, but also because completely neglecting them could potentially increase the risk for injury. Squat patterns don't train the hamstrings effectively and so they do require direct work. A basic hip hinge like an RDL or a 45 degree hyper is a solid start and then pairing that up with a leg curl since one head of the hamstrings doesn't cross the hip and so pure knee flexion is required for complete development. Both seated and lying variations are effective, though seated might have a slight edge because of the added stretch.
