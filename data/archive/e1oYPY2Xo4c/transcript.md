# You could be trying to progress too quickly. Try a smaller jump in weight and match reps with that

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/e1oYPY2Xo4c>

If you have been stuck doing the same number of reps or the same amount of weight on a machine or a free weight exercise, here is how you are going to go about fixing that. There are a few main reasons why people plateau in exercises. One, the stimulus simply isn't enough. You aren't training with enough frequency, with enough volume, with enough intensity. Two, you could actually be doing too much. If you're training with a ton of volume, high frequency, high intensity, not allowing for enough recovery between your sessions, that could actually be suppressing your strength and causing you to plateau. Three, which is a bit more rare since you have to be a bit more advanced as a lifter to have this happen to you, you could have maxed out regional hypertrophy for that muscle. If you're using an exercise that biases a specific region of that muscle, maybe you just maxed out growth for that region. First thing I would suggest for this creator is taking a look at their program. If you're already doing over 10 working sets a week, you may even want to look at reducing that, perhaps between four to six working sets. Doing that twice a week, splitting up the volume, doing anywhere from two to four sets per session. If we're going off the assumption that you're doing too much and you're taking every single set to failure, I would probably look at reducing that, only taking the last set to failure and sticking between one to two RIR for all your other working sets. I'd also suggest training with a larger range of motion. You're cutting off the portion of the ROM where the elbow is most bent and the triceps are most lengthened. The long head of the triceps appears to have better leverage through that range of motion, so it could help with breaking through your plateau as the triceps are now being trained in a novel way and the long head could gain more strength and size through that ROM. I'd also suggest trying to split the volume between a push down and an overhead extension as that is going to bias the triceps differently. Hope this helps.
