# Find a program written by someone that knows what they’re doing and learn how to execute that first

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/c6jdo09ZKDA>

I need you guys to understand that the layout of your split does not matter nearly as much as you think. If you're able to hit the muscles that you're trying to prioritize at least twice a week, then you can use whatever split layout that you want for that based on your preference and what you're able to adhere to in terms of the number days that you're training. So, for example, you could do something like upper lower upper lower with a rest day in between the middle of that. You could do a full body every other day kind of thing if you're able to recover from that and program that well. That is harder to program. You could do a push pull legs rest upper lower split. All of those are great viable splits and one is not necessarily much better than the other. You're going to get similar results from all of those assuming that they're programmed well. When I am building a new program for myself, my clients, my training teams, the first thing that I look at is how many days I'm programming for. Next thing is what muscles am I trying to prioritize within this meso cycle. Then from there, I just plot out which days that I want to put those muscle groups on and then I figure out the rest around that. I see way too many beginner lifters who are worried about the wrong things with trying to learn how to program when really all they should be focusing on right now is just learning how to lift properly, how to execute, set up the exercises, and lift with intensity. Worry about programming once you understand training a bit more and have that knowledge and experience. For now, I recommend going and finding a program. Of course, I have my training teams, but there are a ton out there. Don't try and do it on your own if you don't know what you're doing.
