# Use straps!!

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/L7-z8k3Xc-A>

This may sound controversial, but I don't think that it should be. If you are not using straps on your RDLs, your stiff-leg deadlifts, then you're simply not using enough weight. There is no way that I would be able to RDL your lateral raise max if I was not using straps. And while we're at it, I genuinely don't understand what is so difficult for people with RDLs. It's not a hard exercise to learn. It's something that I teach people in my training teams on a pretty much weekly basis. One of the biggest prerequisites for this exercise is you have to understand how to brace. If you don't know what bracing is, then you shouldn't even be doing this exercise yet. If you know how to brace, great, then you can follow the rest of the cues in this video to help with perfecting your RDL. Keep your chin slightly tucked, then think about pushing your hips back all the way down. Once your hips no longer move back, that is the end of your range of motion. You do not have to touch the plates of the floor for that to be a full ROM. I would also highly suggest walking the bar out of a rack or using a Smith machine, that way you don't have to do a conventional deadlift before you start your set and waste energy. Think about keeping your arms long. Don't try and keep your shoulder blades retracted. That's just going to get pulled out of place as soon as you start your set. To come up, think about pushing your hips into the bar as hard as you can, and I recommend using a controlled eccentric around 3 to 4 seconds, especially if you're not used to the exercise. Once you understand your active range of motion a bit better and you can standardize the range of motion, then you can speed it up a bit.
