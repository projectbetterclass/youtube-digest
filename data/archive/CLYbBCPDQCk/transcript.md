At the base we have the chips, advanced semiconductors like GPUs and TPUs that provide the raw computing power. Without these, training modern AI systems wouldn't be possible. Major players like Nvidia and AMD are at the forefront here, innovating rapidly. While chip investments offer broad exposure to multiple tech sectors, they also face high competition and evolving technology standards. Next are the data centers. These massive facilities host the servers and infrastructure needed to run AI applications. Think of tech giants like Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure. They invest billions in maintaining efficient, scalable, and reliable data centers. While these investments may not get the headlines, they're crucial for the entire AI ecosystems functioning and have relatively stable long-term returns. Then there's the exciting layer of AI model companies. These are the innovators behind the algorithms and architectures that drive AI. With big names like OpenAI and XAI in this space, they capture the imagination with groundbreaking research. However, because there are only a few true model companies and they already command high valuations, the riskreward ratio might be less dramatic compared to earlier stage investments. The fourth layer deals with AI infrastructure, the tools and platforms that deploy these models at scale. This includes everything from middleware frameworks to deployment platforms used by startups and established players like Palunteer. A major debate in venture circles is whether model companies will eventually build their own deployment tools, potentially encroaching on this space. Nonetheless, this layer presents fertile ground for both nimble startups and larger scale innovations with significant growth potential. Finally, we reach the apex AI services. These companies integrate AI into everyday business operations, transforming industries from healthcare billing to finance. For instance, the US healthcare billing industry is a multi-billion dollar space ripe for disruption. By automating processes and reducing inefficiencies, AI services can boost productivity and margins significantly.
