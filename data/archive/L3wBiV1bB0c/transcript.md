# Defending Dr. Mike

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L3wBiV1bB0c>

For those who don't know, a 70minute takedown of Dr. Mike's PhD dissertation was released this week. And while I've been very critical of many things that Mike has said in the past, on this one, I'm going to come to his defense. And I hope you're open-minded to listening. Moments after the original video dropped, fitness creators everywhere seized the opportunity in an attempt to create their own viral moment off of the video. Here's, in my opinion, the issue with this. Every reaction video that has been created simply parrots the message of the original video that Dr. Mike's PhD was a sham and therefore his credibility is too. And in my opinion, this kind of reactive behavior is epically dishonest and intellectually lazy. Why? Well, because none of these reactors appear to have actually looked into anything on their own. They've taken the original video completely at face value. For all we actually know, if you haven't seen the actual paper itself and read it yourself, the way that it was presented could have been misleading. The paper isn't even publicly accessible. It's over a 100 pages long, and it's entirely possible that there were strengths and nuances that were left out of the original critique. Now, let me be very clear. I'm not at all saying that the original critique is baseless. If all of the claims in the original video are actually true, then that says a lot more about the institution of exercise science than it does about Mike's individual credibility. So unless you've read and fully understood the paper yourself, as many of these reactors clearly haven't, you should be very cautious about regurgitating or creating claims that damage someone's reputation. Again, the original video might be exactly spot-on, but the truth is you don't know. And neither do I. And if you're thinking critically about this, you'll realize that it's impossible to make super strong, super confident claims based on someone else's potentially biased interpretation of a paper. Let me be clear about some bigger picture things that I think are as more important here. Number one, if your entire opinion of someone rests solely on the validity of their credentials, then you were never seriously engaging in ideas to begin with. Number two, if in the past you've argued that having a PhD doesn't automatically make someone right, you can't now turn around and say a questionable PhD guarantees that someone is wrong. Those two statements contradict one another. So again, even if the original PhD critique video was completely accurate with no misleading elements whatsoever, that doesn't just suddenly erase every good idea that Mike has ever had or will have. one potentially flawed paper that was written over a decade ago is not necessarily representative of someone's work. Now, number three, if a single YouTube video made with bias changes your entire view of someone, then your view of that person to begin with likely wasn't very deep. Number four, if you've never written or even read or know how to read a PhD dissertation, and yet you are publicly moralizing over someone else's academic work from over a decade ago, you may want to slow down and think more critically about the words you're using. So, here's where I'm going to lead this. Criticism is healthy, and we need it. Holding people accountable is healthy, and we need it. But just simply echoing outrage without actually doing the work yourself isn't criticism. It's just performance. And performance in my opinion has no place in honest discussion.
