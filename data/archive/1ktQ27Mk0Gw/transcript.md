And all the people that I've seen who think that the overhead press is a middle delt exercise all sort of regurgitating the same misinformed opinion that originated from Chris Beardsley where Chris basically recommends that doing shoulder presses below the height of the shoulder is great for the middle delt. Chris was basing this off of a cadaavver based study where they pinned a dead arm up against the pivot point and attempted to measure leverage of the different portions of the delt by attaching a string to the delt tendon and then pulling it and based on that the leverage of the different delts. This is a bad study to use to make certain claims about exercise because the study did not account for rotational position of the arm. In other words, when you're doing an overhead press, your arm has to rotate back. So, the front delt moves to the top side of the joint. So, the front delt is the thing that moves your arm upward. But when your arm is in an internally or neutally rotated position, you're doing that same motion out to the side. There's a completely different stimulus because now the middle delt is on the top of the joint. So, people are basically using a study where they didn't account for this rotation. And assuming that applies to a real human being who has to put their arm like this to push upward, you really want to prioritize your middle delts. I promise you your lateral raises are going to be the best option. Overhead presses are still great, but they're more for the front delt. And the middle delt in that position is really just going to play a stabilizing role.
