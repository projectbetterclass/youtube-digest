# Fix your tempo

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/zbizvs7aNU4>

A lot of people are going about their exercise tempo in the completely wrong way. On one end, there are people who are completely dive-bombing on the eccentric, zero control whatsoever. And then on the other end of that, there are people who are overly controlling the eccentric. And this is not going to be the same for every single exercise. It's going to be dependent on what you're doing. The amount of time that you're spending in the eccentric should be based on how much time you need to control the weight into the lengthened portion of the range of motion for that muscle. For something with a larger range of motion like the bench press or even a squat, for example, or a hinge like a stiff-leg deadlift, you might need 3 to 4 seconds on the way down. But for something like a calf raise, you may only need a couple seconds. If the muscle is not being taken into a big stretch like a lateral raise, for example, then you can also go a bit faster on the eccentric with less risk of injury. Compare that to something like an RDL or stiff-leg deadlift where the muscle is being significantly lengthened, and these are exercises where you definitely want to use a bit more care and time through the eccentric. As a general rule of thumb for you guys, I like around 1 to 2 seconds for exercises that are training the muscle in a more shortened position like a glute bridge, for example, and I like around 3 to 4 seconds for exercises where the muscle is being significantly lengthened and you're training it in that position. Then as for the concentric like the up on the glute bridge here, you want to be as forceful as possible while still being controlled.
