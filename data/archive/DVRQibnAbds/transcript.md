# Why “stop doing this” doesn’t work

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/DVRQibnAbds>

One of the least effective ways to get someone to do an exercise differently is to tell them to stop doing what they're doing or to say something like, "Hey, don't do that. Do this." When you say to someone who doesn't know that they're doing something to stop doing that thing, they have no clue what you're talking about. So, example, if someone is doing a row and you want to get them to target their lats. You want to You want to get them to shove their armpit kind of down in in this way, right? And instead, maybe they're pulling and they're like They're They're They're like doing this shrugging thing. Instead of saying, "Hey, stop shrugging." say something more like, "Pull your arm down into your pocket." The reason that you would want to lean toward a positive cue rather than a negative cue is again because if someone is pulling up like this into their traps, they don't know that they're doing it. They have no awareness to begin with of what they're doing that you want them to stop doing. So, my bias is to lean into at least to begin with get people to do what you want, elbow down to the side, squeeze the shoulder to the pocket, whatever it happens to be, over what you don't want them to do. So, that's why I lean toward the positive cueing. In other words, lead them in the direction that you do want and show them how to do that because that's much easier than trying to get someone to avoid something they don't know is happening to begin with.
