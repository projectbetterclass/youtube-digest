# Slower Negatives = Faster Gains?

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/YRGBDECgT2g>

Everyone tells you that lowering the weight slowly is better for building muscle. Slow, slow, slow. >> But is that actually true? Researchers recently looked at nine different studies comparing slower negatives in the 3 to 6second range to faster ones done in just 1 to 2 seconds. The review included 166 lifters performing a variety of exercises and measured their gains using ultrasound and MRI scans. What was the result? Well, despite all the hype around time under tension, the slower eccentrics produced no extra growth compared to the faster ones. A slower tempo might feel harder, but feeling harder doesn't always mean building more muscle. Now, this doesn't mean that you should just let the weight free fall. Controlling the negative is still important to keep your reps consistent and to reduce injury risk, but going super slow and stretching it much beyond 4 to 5 seconds, that's probably overkill. What really matters for muscle growth is training close to failure with solid form and progressing the load over time. Tempo matters, but it's a much smaller factor than most people think.
