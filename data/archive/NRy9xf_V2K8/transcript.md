# If you're not getting sore after training...

[music] >> If you really think you can maximize your gains without getting sore after your workouts, you're correct. See, most people think that soreness comes from muscle damage and so it must mean they had a productive workout. But muscle damage isn't a driver of muscle growth and muscle soreness doesn't appear to be a result of muscle damage anyway. Delayed onset muscle soreness seems to come from a mix of factors like local nerve irritation and connective tissue strain and it tends to show up most when you perform movements your body isn't accustomed to. That's why someone can bench 315 and barely feel sore after a heavy chest workout, but then do their first yoga session and feel like they got hit by a bus the next day. Now yeah, if you're still a novice and you literally never get sore at all, that's probably a sign you're not training hard enough. But overall, soreness is a very poor gauge for muscle growth. I personally get very little to no soreness most of the time and TrainWithUs members frequently report the same thing despite making faster progress than they did on higher volume workouts that left them sore for days after. The only way to know your workouts are truly stimulating real gains is if you're consistently adding weight and reps to your lifts over time. That's what your training should be centered around and when your workouts are properly structured to maximize the stimulus without unnecessary fatigue, soreness typically won't be much of a factor.
