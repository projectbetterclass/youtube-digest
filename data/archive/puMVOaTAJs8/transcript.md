# Just because someone has a good physique doesn't mean you shouldn't listen to them

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/puMVOaTAJs8>

Just because somebody has a good physique, it does not mean that you should listen to them. I recently saw a video of one of the top-level IFBB pro bodybuilders talking about what they're doing in the last weeks of their prep, where they're going to be increasing intensity in the gym with more intensifiers like rest-pause sets, drop sets, things of that nature. This is the opposite of what I would suggest for just about anyone who's in a cutting phase, especially in the last weeks of contest prep, but if we're just talking about a general cutting phase for the average person, I still would not suggest it. In the last weeks of your cut, in the second half of your cut, where you're the leanest that you've been, you have the least amount of food, maybe the least energy that you've had throughout your cut, it's not typically a good idea to start ramping up intensity. Going from bulk or maintenance to cutting, I don't even suggest changing your training program. I don't think that's typically needed unless you're running a very high-volume program, but I definitely don't suggest increasing volume going into your cut. Why would you go from lower to higher volume when you had more energy to work with previously? [music] During the back end of a cut or a contest prep, you're just trying to maintain as much muscle as possible, so it doesn't make sense to add in a bunch of intensifiers or ramp up intensity. If anything on the back end of a prep or a cut, I would suggest lowering volume to maintenance volume, that way you can just focus on maintaining muscle and preserving as much energy as possible.
