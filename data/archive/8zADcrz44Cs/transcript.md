# Most People Skip These Important Forearm Exercises

Most people do wrist extensions and wrist curls for their forearms, but they neglect these two motions. These motions are called pronation and supenation. Pronation occurs when the radius bone folds over the ulna bone. Supenation occurs when the opposite happens. So, in order to load this motion, we basically just need a resistance that is pulling the wrist in either direction. The first and most simple option you could use is a dumbbell supenation or a dumbbell pronation. All you need to do is lie sideways on a bench, grab a single dumbbell, grab one end of it, and basically just twist your hand in either direction. Now, while this is super convenient, it might be load limiting because of the fact that you have to hold the dumbbell, and it might just be uncomfortable to do anyway. Now, here are two options that I like way more than that. In order to train pronation, what you're going to do is set a cable at the bottom of its rack. Then, grab a d handle and fold the Dandle over itself so that you're able to actually twist it and move the cable. From there, make sure you have a relatively tight grip on the handle. Put your opposite hand underneath of your forearm. Perform the pronations that way. Remember, you want to think about this purely as spinning. You don't want to try to move your hand all over the place. It should just be like there's a center point around which you're pivoting. And then the train supernation, all you got to do is the opposite. So move the cable all the way up to the top of the stack. Keep that same sort of fold over the d handle that you had before. Now this time you want the pinky side of the hand being pulled up to the ceiling. You want to put your non-working hand on top of the forearm now because the cable is trying to pull your arm upward. And in the same way that you did before, all you're doing is just twisting your forearm around. Now, if the D-h handle option feels a little bit clunky for you because some people just have a tough time setting it up, instead what you can use is just a normal rope. You can grab the middle of it if that's more comfortable. You can also grab the end of it. But either way, the concepts are the same. You're grabbing something that is trying to twist your wrist in one direction against which you are reversing that direction. And training these kinds of motions at least once a week can make a big difference in the strength and size of your forearms.
