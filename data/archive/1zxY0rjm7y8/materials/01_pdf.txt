Why	discount	rates	could	(and	should)	
change	over	time:	Firm	specific
¨ Necessary,	not	optional:	Adjusting	discount	rates	across	time	
may	seem	like	a	needless	complication	but	it	is	a	necessary	
one,	if	you	want	your	valuation	to	remain	internally	
consistent.	
¨ Has	to	change	with	changes	in	company:	If	you	are	assuming	
changes	in	your	company	characteristics	(growth,	business	
mix,	geographical	exposure)	in	your	cash	flows,	as	you	move	
through	time,	you	should	be	changing	the	discount	rate	to	
reflect	these	changes.	
¨ Greater	effect	at	young,	transitional	companies:	While	this	is	
true	for	all	companies,	the	effect	will	be	greater	when	you	are	
valuing	young	companies	or	companies	in	transition,	where	
you	expect	large	changes	in	the	company	as	you	move	
through	your	forecast	period.
5
Why	discount	rates	could	(and	should)	
change	over	time:	Macro
¨ Macro	inputs	can	change	as	well:	If	you	are	open	to	the	
possibility	that	discount	rates	can	change	over	time,	you	
can	also	then	bring	in	your	perspectives	on	the	macro	
inputs	in	the	discount	rate	into	your	forecasts.
¨ Risk	free	Rates:	With	the	risk	free,	for	example,	you	
could	bring	in	an	assumption	that	the	rate	will	rise	or	fall	
over	time	or	even	incorporate	a	term	structure	of	risk	
free	rates	into	your	computations.
¨ Risk	Premiums:	With	the	risk	premiums	(ERP	and	default	
spread),	you	can	adjust	for	changes	that	you	expect	to	
occur	especially	at	the	country	risk	level.

The	Bottom	Line
¨ A	DCF	where	the	discount	rate	remains	constant	
over	time	should	be	more	the	exception	than	the	
rule	because	it	works	only	if	you	assume	that	the	
company	that	you	are	valuing	will	not	change	over	
time	(in	business	mix,	geography	and	debt	ratio).
¨ Allowing	discount	rates	to	change	over	time	keeps	
your	valuations	internally	consistent	and	reduces	the	
pressure	on	you	to	get	it	right.
