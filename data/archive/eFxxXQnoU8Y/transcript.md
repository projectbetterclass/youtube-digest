# What No One Knows About Pull-Downs

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eFxxXQnoU8Y>

Many people seem to think that pull downs are a great exercise to train the traps, but this just isn't true. Here's why. There are three portions of the traps. The upper portion, the middle portion, and the lower portion. All three of these traps create upward rotation when they contract. And upward rotation of the shoulder blade occurs whenever your shoulder blade tips in the upward direction, like when you raise your arms above your head. And so now you might be thinking, Ben, are you stupid? Your arms go above your head whenever you do a pull down. So obviously your traps need to contribute, right? Yes, your arms do go above your head in a pull down, but no. Still wrong. Whenever you do a pull down motion, the cable pulls your arm upward overhead. But because we are resisting the bar downward throughout the entire range of motion, we're actually creating a force known as downward rotation. And downward rotation is exactly what it sounds like. It's just the opposite of upward rotation. So even when our arms are being pulled upward and overhead, we are still creating a downward rotation force against the bar no matter what throughout the entire range of motion. And this downward rotation force is the exact opposite of what the traps create. Just think of this in a different way. Would you say that your triceps contribute to a biceps curl just because when you lower your arm, your elbow is straightening? I certainly hope not. So the same thing applies here. Your shoulder blades rotate upward as the weight stack itself lowers, but your traps aren't the things doing the work. And now you might be thinking, Ben, I swear I feel those muscles between my shoulder blades. What the hell is going on? Am I crazy? Actually, no. That's supposed to happen. Here's why. The reason that you do feel something between your shoulder blades is because there's another muscle which actually sits underneath of the traps. Actually, two, the romboid major and the romboid minor. And when both of the rhomboids contract, they create a downward rotation force. In other words, they help to rotate the shoulder blade downward and upward in either part of a pull down motion. And this is all because you are creating that downward rotation force throughout the entire range of motion. And along with the romboids, the lats, the tererry's major, and the rear delt all contribute significantly to the pull down. So pull downs do train your midback, just not your traps, only those two romboid muscles. And if you do want to train your traps directly, your bread and butter should be any kind of overhead motion like a raise or a press, any kind of shrugging motion, and any kind of row motion. So pull downs are obviously an awesome exercise, just not for the traps.
