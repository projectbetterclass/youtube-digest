# Why don’t you stand out?

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/h9gI-1r7mHE>

Hey, so have you also noticed that so much content and specifically fitness content just looks exactly the same now? Like, what's up with that? What's going on? And what if the reason that everyone's fitness content looks exactly the same is because everyone's trying so hard to be different. And what if the secret to creating something truly original is actually to stop trying to stand out altogether? Here's one perspective. Years ago it felt to me like social media was at least somewhat personal, right? Like when you followed someone, you got to know them because you actually saw their stuff and also because you found them probably following your own interests. The personal, the original, the genuine, all these things were highly highly encouraged as a consequence and this made pages feel like small communities of people who were deeply curious about the same things. It felt alive, it felt collaborative, and this collaboration inspired more creativity in a positive feedback loop. But then something seemed to change. I don't know what, but social media now feels like it's a place where most of what you see actually comes from people you don't and won't know, right? And the perceived benefit of this is basically that people can go viral and grow much more easily, but I wonder if it's also changed fundamentally what motivates people to actually post in the first place, right? Because now, rather than people asking themselves, "Oh, what am I genuinely interested in exploring?" people instead ask, "What can I do that will get attention?" And when your primary interest is solely in getting attention, what do you do? Well, you create this veneer of interest, say in fitness, when your actual interest is solely just to stand out for the sake of itself, right? Which makes for empty content that's not only uncreative, but also that seems to kill other people's creativity when they see it. Now, ironically, the desire to stand out is itself the thing which gets in the way of being truly unique. It's the same as the desire to escape from yourself so that you can be who you think others want you to be, right? And trying to be original requires you to constantly reference what other people are doing, but genuine originality might actually just emerge from precisely what you do when you stop referencing other people. So, I'll leave you with a single question. What would you do? What would you create if the sole purpose of doing so was actually to just explore what you're interested in?
