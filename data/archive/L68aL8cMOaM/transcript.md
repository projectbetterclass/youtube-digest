People end up moving their spines too much when they don't maintain contact with the thigh pad and the handles. If you make sure that your quad stays pinned to the pad throughout the entirety of the range, especially toward the bottom, and you make sure that you pull on the handles, especially as the weight is lowering, you won't lose contact with either of those main two anchors, and as a result, you won't actually start to arch the back to try to stabilize the motion. In addition, because this is an arcing motion, the direction of our intent should actually change throughout the range. So, at the beginning of the motion when the pad is pushing down, we need to be pushing up, but at the top of the motion when the pad is actually pushing backward, we need to be thinking about moving this way. This is also for the same reason that at the bottom you should really focus on the thigh restraint, and then as you get toward the top, you need to focus on the handle restraint, right? because this motion is occurring and that handle is perfect for us resisting sliding backward, and as this motion is occurring, this pad is perfect for resisting us moving upward or downward.
