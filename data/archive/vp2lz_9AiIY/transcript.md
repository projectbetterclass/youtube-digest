# 15 year old lifting transformation

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/vp2lz_9AiIY>

This was me at 15 years old after just 2 years of lifting. And even though that was almost 25 years ago, the three main things that got me these results still apply today. The first is steroids. No, I'm just kidding. I wasn't even taking creatine at that time because my mom wouldn't let me, but no. The first is that even at that age, I trained really hard. And after all my years in the gym now, I can say with certainty that the biggest difference between the people who make mediocre gains versus outstanding gains is their intensity. Everyone loves arguing about optimal volume, frequency, splits, exercises, but at the end of the day, it's the people in the gym making ugly faces and involuntary noises on those last couple hard grindy reps that are usually the most jacked. Number two, I tracked everything. Every set of every exercise. I always walked into the gym knowing exactly what I did last time and what I was aiming for this time. Now, it's true that you can't force progressive overload since your strength today is a result of what you did in the previous session, but you can control the mindset you bring. Showing up and thinking, "Well, maybe I'll progress today, maybe I won't. Just trust the process." No, you should be going in there with the goal of improving in some way. You won't be stronger on every lift every time. Yes, you'll have off days. I'm not telling you to ego lift, but the person who shows up intending to beat the logbook is the one I would bet on every time. And number three, I was ruthlessly consistent. I remember training legs outside in the snow because my only available squat rack was two big rocks in my backyard. I remember going to the gym late at night to do deadlifts after rugby games, totally exhausted with mud still in my ears. Was that always necessary? Probably not, but this whole trend of just listen to your body gets abused way too often. Yeah, sometimes you genuinely need rest, but most of the time it's just straight-up cope your mind comes up with to keep you comfortable. There were plenty of days I didn't want to train, but showed up anyway, and there wasn't a single workout I ever regretted no matter how I felt beforehand.
