# Dry humping the bar is not progressive overload

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/24luXBv-hK8>

I'm sorry to tell you this, but if you had to aggressively dry hump the bar to squeeze out those last couple reps on your curls, that doesn't count as progressive overload. You're not building more muscle that way. You're just violating the equipment and making everyone around you slightly uncomfortable. Yeah, progressive overload should be the main focus of your program. And steadily adding weight and reps over time is how you know you're actually making gains. Size causes strength, and since muscle growth happens so slowly and can't be measured in the short term, tracking your strength gains is the most reliable metric. But it only counts as real progression if your technique stays consistent. If you start using more momentum, speeding up your eccentrics, or cutting the range of motion, you're not actually getting stronger. You're just throwing around heavier weights with worse execution. So, take a breath, chill. You don't need to add weight every week on every lift. Focus on small, gradual increases while keeping your form dialed in and let it slowly stack up over time.
