# There's no such thing as 'correct biomechanics'

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/52s69rLb4IE>

There is no such thing as correct biomechanics, nor is there any such thing as correct posture. For example, is it correct for the shoulder blade to be here, or what about right there? What about right there? What happens if it looks like it's winging a little bit? What happens if it looks like it's closer to the spine? Which of those is correct and why? What about from this perspective? Is it correct for the shoulder blade to be more forward or more backward? Or are you still dogmatically attached to something that's making you think that the shoulder should be a particular way? Cool, then look at a different example. What's the correct way to position the hand when you're in a standing position? Is it toward the body? Is it away from the body? Is it up to the ceiling? Is it somewhere in between? What is the correct position to put your [ __ ] hand? There are no correct biomechanics or positions. There are only those positions the individual either responds in the way that we want them to or not. If a position or movement doesn't feel good, it's not because it's wrong. It's simply because there's a way in which the position or movement doesn't fit the individual. So, there's simply just what happens to the individual during an exercise or a position. This idea of right or wrong is just an unnecessary layer on top of what happens that's essentially a judgment to try to make sense of what's happening. So, see what it's like to just approach your lifting and the way that your body looks as not right or wrong, but just as what it is. And what you'll find is that the freedom that comes from this in terms of the exercises that you can do, your ability to navigate training, and your ability as a coach to coach other people, all these things improve dramatically and very rapidly because you start to see solution sets that you just didn't before because the old model of thinking that you had was overly constraining and frankly inaccurate.
