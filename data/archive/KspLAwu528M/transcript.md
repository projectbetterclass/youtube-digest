In machine learning, this is called the grounding problem. A model trained purely on text learns the relationship between words, not the relationships between things. When you ask GPT what happens if you drop a glass on concrete, it knows the answer because 10 million people wrote about it before, but the model has never felt gravity. It reasons by analogy to language, not by experience of physics. For most tasks, writing, coding, research, analysis, this doesn't matter. Language is good enough. Except, it becomes a crisis the moment you want AI to do anything in the physical world. Why? Because the physical world doesn't run on analogies. The gap between Tesla's physical data flywheel and anyone else is time. That one isn't catchable as quickly. Synthetic data is getting better. Researchers inside Open AI argue that world simulators will eventually be good enough to replace physical data entirely. If you can simulate the physics of a robot gripping a deformable object accurately enough, you don't need a factory full of robots doing it in the world. You just run the simulation a billion times. I think that argument has a genuine hole in it. The world is messier than any simulator has managed to capture so far. The gap between a simulated grip and a physical grip is that the world has failure modes nobody programmed. A robot in a actual factory encounters unexpected friction, unexpected vibrations, unexpected humans who slightly moves a component. Simulations can generate variations on known edge cases. It cannot generate edge cases it hasn't been told to generate. This distinction between edge cases you can simulate and edge cases that only emerge from actual operation is the core of why the physical data advantage is so difficult to buy your way around. Physical operation generates the edge cases that break your simulator. That's the loop. You don't know what you don't know until you put a robot in a room with actual objects and actual physics and actual humans and it fails in a way your simulation never predicted. That failure becomes training data. The simulator gets updated but the initial discovery only happens in the physical world. Tesla aims to make those discoveries at scale as deployments expand. The gap between we have a great simulator and we have a great simulator plus 10 million hours of robot operation, that is a challenge for companies without large-scale physical data collection systems like OpenAI. The model trained on that data is downstream of someone else's choices. The partner, knowing what that data is worth, will make sure the licensing terms reflect it.
