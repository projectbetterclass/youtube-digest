Have you ever wondered why you feel so much of your foot instead of your calves whenever you're doing calf raises? Whenever we're doing calf raises on a surface that isn't able to actually arc with our foot, our feet actually have to accommodate for that surface and as a consequence, the whole foot has to start to bend. And since the calves only really create and manipulate this kind of a motion, you'll tend to feel them a lot less the more that this stuff up here gets involved. So in order to limit this involvement, you want to make sure that the part of your foot that's on the platform is actually below the balls of the feet. And in addition, you want to make sure that towards the bottom of the range that your feet aren't climbing the platform and that you're allowing your toes to come up so that the entire foot can stay mostly rigid especially toward the bottom of the range of motion. I personally tend to limit range so that my foot stays mostly parallel to the surface and then I just come up on my toes from there. If you have the opportunity to use a machine that actually has a foot plate that arcs with the foot, you'll find that you don't actually have as much sensation through your toes because the foot doesn't have an opportunity to bend. When the foot plate moves with the entire length of the foot, the foot stays rigid and we just get motion at the ankle where the calves would be the things that are acting rather than all the muscles underneath and on top of the foot.
