If you've been grinding away on calf raises but still get mogged by 110 lb women who've never trained them a day in their life, this study might explain what you're doing wrong. Researchers had participants perform standing calf raises with one leg and seated calf raises with the other. Here's what they found after 12 weeks. Growth in the soius was about the same in both legs, but in the gastromus, the bigger, more visible calf muscle, it wasn't even close. The standing calf raise grew it by about 10% while the seated calf rays produced almost no gains at all. Why? Because unlike the soius, the gastromus crosses the knee joint. It's not just a plantar flexor, it's also a knee flexor. So when your knees are bent, the muscle is placed in a pre-shortened position where it can't generate significant force or experience much mechanical tension.
