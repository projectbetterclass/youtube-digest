## 1. [02:26] Educational slide explaining brokerage fees - definition and purpose

BROKERAGE FEES. You pay a fee to your broker when you buy or sell shares. This fee is the payment for the broker executing your buy/sell orders.

## 2. [04:50] Text slide with S&P 500 historical performance data

The S&P 500 Index originally began in 1926 as the 'composite index' comprised of only 90 stocks. Average annual return since inception in 1926 through 2018 is approximately 10%-11%. Average annual return since adopting 500 stocks into the index in 1957 through 2018 is roughly 8%.

## 3. [09:30] Educational slide defining capital gains

CAPITAL GAINS. When you sell your shares, the gain is added on top of your taxable income for that year.

## 4. [10:12] Educational slide about capital gains tax strategy

CAPITAL GAINS. If you're looking at realising a large capital gain, it is worth considering whether you should hold it for longer than 12 months, to get the 50% discount.

## 5. [11:03] Educational slide about FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) in investing

NO FOMO. It's okay to research for many months before making any decisions. It's better to know what you're getting yourself into, rather than rushing due to FOMO and making a big mistake.

## 6. [11:19] Bar chart showing investment growth over time - compound interest visualization

Results. Y-axis: Savings (0, 25k, 50k, 75k, 100k). X-axis: Years (0-45). Chart demonstrates exponential growth over 45 years.

## 7. [12:36] Educational slide about capital losses and tax strategy

CAPITAL LOSSES. If you've made a large capital gain and another one of your investments is looking like a capital loss, you can realise that loss deliberately to reduce your taxable gain.

## 8. [14:09] Educational slide listing examples of speculative investment behavior

SPECULATING. 'Frank told me this share is going to the moon this week, so I bought in!' 'I like cars so I bought Ford stock' 'TikTok is the next big thing so I'm buying'

## 9. [14:35] Educational slide listing key principles for sound investment practices

INVESTMENT. Understand the business. Understand how the business makes money and what the cash flows are. Understand the debt load of the business and its ability to repay.

## 10. [15:41] Educational slide explaining SEC Form 13F filings

WHAT IS A 13F FILING? The Securities and Exchange Commission's (SEC) Form 13F is a quarterly report that is required to be filed by all institutional investment managers with at least $100 million in assets under management. It discloses their equity holdings and can provide some insights into what the smart money is doing in the market.
