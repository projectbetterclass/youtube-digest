All right, so people are always asking me, "Ben, what's the difference between doing a split squat with one dumbbell in your hand versus when you have two dumbbells in your hands and you're doing them two at the same time?" So, if you have one dumbbell at a time, what you're going to notice is as you lower down into that motion, your body is naturally going to twist into the working side leg. Because you're forced to kind of dip the opposite side of your pelvis toward the working leg, you're actually going to stretch the outer glutes a little bit more. So, if you think about it on this side, right, you put your right leg forward, you're going to naturally be twisting into that right leg and stretch the upper right glute max more. And in addition, some of the back half of the side glutes. But, when you're using two dumbbells, they basically balance each other out, assuming they're a similar weight or something like that, and you just stay centered over your legs. So, the lower glute max becomes more involved when you're holding two dumbbells because you don't have that same twisting demand, at least you're not being forced into it by the dumbbell. As a consequence, the two-leg dumbbell split squat tends to be a little bit more quad involved, whereas if you use a single dumbbell and you do allow that twisting toward the working leg, you're going to involve proportionately less quad and more upper outer glute.
