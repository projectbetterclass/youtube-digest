Is lifting with a rounded back bad? Well, it's first worth acknowledging that this is a bad question. And it's a bad question because it assumes that the answer could be a simple yes or no in all contexts. So, let's change the question. In absolute, is lifting with a rounded back inherently dangerous for your spine? The answer is no. Definitely not. In fact, lifting with a rounded back is the very thing that will make your back more resilient in the rounded position. Think about it this way. If someone came up to you in the gym as you were doing biceps curl and said, "Bro, don't do that. It's bad for your elbow to bend your elbow," you would probably think that was a joke. And you'd be right to think so. And the same applies to lifting with a rounded back on an inherent level. I personally like to do deadlifts where the goal is to lift as much weight as possible with a little bit of a rounded back just simply because I'm painfree in that position and I'm also the strongest in that position. But does this mean that rounding your back is good for all goals and all people? The answer is clearly no. Example number one. If you wanted to make a hinge motion more hip dominant, you'd want to predominantly just isolate the hip hinging without moving your spine as much. If you're someone who has severe back pain in a rounded back deadlifting position, but zero pain in a neutral spine position, it's probably wise to avoid the rounded back position, at least for the time being, so that you can continue to lift pain-free. This isn't to say that you just avoid rounded back lifting forever, but in this specific context, it might be wise to grade exposure back to that position slowly and use positions and continue to lift pain-free.
