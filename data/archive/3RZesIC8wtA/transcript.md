# How are pull-ups and chin-ups different?

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/3RZesIC8wtA>

What are the differences between a pull up and a chin up? When we do a pull up and our hands face away from us, our arms naturally move toward a wider position. As a consequence of the wider position, muscle recruitment tends to lean in the direction of the upper back as opposed to the lats. When we turn our hands toward our face to do a chin up, our arms naturally move more in front of our body and narrow to the rib cage. As a consequence of this, the lats will contribute proportionately more, and in addition, because the palms are supinated, the biceps will be involved to a higher degree. In addition, doing a chin up allows you to move your arms in front of the body. As a consequence, you can move more purely vertically. Whereas, when you do a pull up, a lot of the times people's arms have to move more backward than downward. This is often why you see people's chests get pulled up to the ceiling. And both of these factors contribute to the chin up being more lat biased and the pull up being more upper back dominant. Both the pull up and the chin up are fantastic exercises to train the back, but which one you choose will depend on what your goals are predominantly.
