Let's just jump straight into this. Number one, squats are a hamstrings exercise. No, they're definitely not. People still believe this falsely because the hamstrings cross the back side of the hip joint. And this means that they can create what's known as hip extension, which is in essence just pushing yourself up out of the bottom of a squat. But here's the thing, the hamstrings also cross the back side of the knee, which means that they can bend the knee or create knee flexion like in any leg curl. But we all know that squats of any kind are a quad exercise because you have to straighten your knee when you're pushing through the floor. So even though the hamstrings do cross the backside of the hip and they can create hip extension, they cannot contribute substantially to pushing us out of the bottom of the squat because if they did, they would literally prevent the knee from straightening. And to put it even more simply, the hamstrings act directly against the quads. And since we need to use the quads in a squat, the hamstrings, even though they do cross the back of the hip, will prevent the knee from straightening if they contract too hard. So your hamstrings play a stability role. And if you're still feeling something along the inner side of your thigh, that's just your adductor magnus muscle, which along with the glute max is a prime mover in any squat variation that you do. Many people understandably confuse sensation in their adductor magnus with sensation of the hamstrings because locationally they're very close together. Number two, deeper is always better. As a hard rule, this simply isn't true. Squatting deeper is good for some goals and bad for other goals. For example, if you wanted to target your quads with a squat, it would make sense to drop your butt to your heels and to try to bend your knees as much as possible down into the bottom position. This would make it so that you could get a maximal stretch on the quads all the while imposing the most amount of resistance on them in that position. But this technique by definition unloads the hips. In other words, the glute max and the adductor magnus. And performing a squat that looks more like a deadlift, what many people call a half squat is actually much much better for targeting the glutes and the adductor. Why? Well, because the hips get fully bent in this position and the load is far away from the hips such that there's maximal loading on the hips and maximal stretch on those hip muscles in that position just like we talked about with the quads. So, this kind of half squat takes away load from the quads and redistributes them to the hips which can be a absolutely good thing in the context of trying to say train your glutes. So, if targeting your glutes is your goal, do not assume that deeper is better. deeper might be better if your goals are to train more your quads, but you need to start by defining what it is you're trying to do with the squat in the first place to actually know. Number three, squats are the king of all lower body exercises. This is just totally false and it comes from the emotional and pretty much outdated opinions and emotional whims of old school traditional training meats. Nothing nothing's like squats. You can do leg presses, but why? Why do an inferior movement? Why do a movement that looks good that produces hardly any results? While squats can absolutely be an awesome exercise for many people, they are by no means the king of lower body exercises in the same way that no single exercise is the king of all exercises in a category. Viewing any exercise in this way is totally unhelpful. Imagine if you said apples or bananas are the king of all fruits. It's just an opinion. It's not a fact. And it actually makes a difference and prevents people from understanding these things because they just assume that some exercises have inherent qualities that make them better. In reality, the exercises that are best for you are the ones that are comfortable and feel good and that accomplish your specific goal. And honestly, if you wanted to rank the back squat on the spectrum of how good it is at targeting your legs only, there are many other exercises like machine-based pressing exercises that are much more specific to targeting your legs, like a leg press or a belt squat or a hack squat. These other exercises don't require really any demand on your spine, and so your erectors and your abs aren't having to do a ton of work, and you can really just focus on muscles of your legs with these machine-based movements. So, if anything, the back squat is pretty far down on the list of the best lower body exercises because it involves the management of so many other variables like technique and balance that these other machine-based exercises simply don't.
