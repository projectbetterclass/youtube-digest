Optimizing Debt: Earnings as a Driver
¨ In the cost of capital and adjusted present value approaches, it is a 
company’s capacity to generate operating income that determines 
it debt capacity. 
¨ With negative or very low operating income, the benefits of debt 
dissipate, because
¤ There are no tax benefits from interest expenses without the income to 
offset them against. 
¤ The synthetic ratings collapses quickly, as interest coverage ratios turn 
negative.
¤ The cost of equity also rises faster, since the levered beta does not get the 
partial protection that taxes offer .
¨ If you are wondering why it is not EBITDA, but EBIT , that 
determines optimal debt ratio, a company that is a going concern 
with growing earnings will have to reinvest the DA back as cap ex to 
keep itself going.

Using last year’s earnings
¨ When computing optimal debt ratios, it is natural to 
latch on to the most recent year’s financial statements, 
and use the operating income from that year . After all, it 
is the most updated value that you have on that metric.
¨ This can create problems with
¤ Commodity or Cyclical companies, where earnings go through 
cycles of down and up years.
¤ Companies that had a really good or bad year, for transient 
reasons.
¤ Companies that play operating income games, moving non 
operating income into the operating income column or claiming 
non operating expenses as operating expenses.
4
With cyclical companies, it is best to determine 
debt capacity with normal income
¨ With cyclical companies, earnings will track the economy, 
looked abnormally high in boom years and abnormally low or 
negative in recession years. 
¨ Since you are making a judgment on debt capacity for the 
long term, you should be borrowing on income that you can 
generate across a cycle, not just the most recent year.
¨ To normalize, 
¤ Go back over time to cover an entire cycle, or better still, more than 
one economic cycle.
¤ You can average across the cycle, using
n Average absolute earnings, for firms that are stable
n Average pre-tax operating margin for firms which are growing or 
shrinking. Your normalized income can be estimated by multiplying 
revenues in the most recent year by the pre-tax operating margin.

With commodity companies, it is trickier ..
¨ If you believe that commodity cycles are short and 
predictable, you can use the same normalization 
approach that you would use for cyclical companies, 
either averaging earnings or margins over a cycle.
¨ If you feel that commodity cycles are very long term 
and/or unpredictable, you can do one of two things:
¤ Compute operating income that you would have at a 
“conservative” commodity price, normalizing earnings to reflect 
that commodity price.
¤ Compute operating income at today’s commodity price, 
compute an optimal based on that number, and then build a 
buffer by setting your actual below the optimal.

With companies playing operating 
earnings games..
¨ If an extraordinary or one-time item is causing the 
earnings to be too low or too high, estimate the earnings 
without that item and compute the optimal debt ratio, 
with the re-estimated operating income.
¨ If the item that is distorting earnings is not one-time, but 
is causing distortions every year, re-compute the 
earnings without that item over time and us the 
normalization approaches mentioned earlier .
¨ One simple way of checking is to compare the company’s 
margins to the sector averages and if they are wildly 
different, you may consider using the sector average or a 
number closer to it, in computing the optimal.
