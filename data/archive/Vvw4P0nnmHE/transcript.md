# People still don’t have a good understanding of what makes an exercise a good choice for hypertrophy

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Vvw4P0nnmHE>

I never understood why people use the Smith machine to do bench or incline press. Just get under the barbell and lift like a man. Why is everything becoming so soft? Well, today is your lucky day because I'm happy to explain. There is nothing special about doing a free weight press over a machine press. A lot of people equate free weight pressing with being more manly and resulting in more gains, perhaps due to the feeling that it's more difficult since there is more of a stability demand. But, that stability demand actually makes the press a bit worse for hypertrophy considering now your body has to do more work to stabilize the exercise and therefore take away recruitment from the muscles that you're trying to train. As you become more advanced as a lifter, it becomes even more important to utilize exercises that are more stable and have better bracing. That way you can maximize motor unit recruitment. And it's also easier for beginner to intermediate lifters to make their working sets more effective using machines since they don't have to worry about coordinating and stabilizing the exercises like they would with free weights. All of the data, the research that we have on free weights versus machines for hypertrophy show that you're going to grow a similar amount of muscle no matter which you pick. The longer that you do a free weight exercise, the better that you get at coordinating it, which is why we see similar growth between free weights and machines. With machines, however, there's much less of a learning curve. If you're setting it up properly and pushing close to failure, then you'll see much better growth out of that than you would a free weight press that you're having a hard time stabilizing.
