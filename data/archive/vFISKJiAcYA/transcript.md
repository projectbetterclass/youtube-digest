# Trapezius muscle - complete anatomy & training guide

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/vFISKJiAcYA>

Here's a simple guide on how to understand and train all of the different portions of the traps. The upper traps run from the upper spine and skull to the clavicle and shoulder blade. What this means is that they can move the shoulder blade vertically and also rotate it upwardly whenever we need to take our arm over our head. To train the upper traps, we can perform some kind of vertical shrug or some kind of lateral raise. The middle traps attach a little bit lower and run more horizontally just onto the shoulder blade. What this means is that they can move the scapula toward the spine, but they can also still elevate the shoulder girdle a little bit. So, to train the middle traps, what we can do is something like a row or something like a shrug at a slightly more horizontal angle. The lower traps run from slightly lower on the spine to the edge of the scapula. Because they attach to the inner edge, they play a strong role in upper rotation. They can also potentially play a role in moving the scapula toward the spine along with the middle and upper traps. So, to train the lower traps, any kind of lateral raise type motion is sufficient, but rows and pull-downs will also train these fibers.
