# People are confused about bench press!

I got called a [ __ ] for saying that you don't need to touch your chest when you barbell bench. I also was called a little [ __ ] a big People also said things like the world's getting too soft. And also someone said that if you can't touch your chest, then you probably shouldn't be benching at all. You're completely welcome to think that I'm a [ __ ] or a [ __ ] but I want to clear up the confusion about the other comments. Saying that if you can't touch your chest that you shouldn't be benching at all is pretty silly. For example, if someone can't squat fully ass to grass, does that make a squat that's 5°, 10° short of ass to grass a complete waste of time? And if so, why would that be the case? You're doing a pull-up and got your chin just below the bar, would it make sense to say the other 90% of the range of motion is a complete waste of time? If you did a deadlift and instead of lifting it off of the floor, you started from top down and cut the range of motion, would that be a waste of time or would we just call that an RDL? The reality is, outside of powerlifting where you have to compete, the standard to touch your chest in any barbell bench press is totally arbitrary. It should be based on the individual and whether or not that's actually comfortable for them with the given technique and the given load that's selected. Now, on the opposite side of the aisle, people are also saying things like this where if you do touch your chest, then your shoulders are just going to be demolished. This is as short-sided as people who are on the flip side of this. There's no reason to think that just because you're touching your chest, your shoulders are automatically going to have a really hard time with it. Reality is, clearly, some people are completely fine with it and others are completely not fine with it. And this goes for every single exercise that you do. Range of motion should totally depend on you and your goals and what you're comfortable with right now. And just as a final point, I did see a lot of comments that also asked the question, well, how could you possibly measure your progress if you're not touching your chest? I think this is a good faith intelligent question to ask, but I think that it's misguided in a very important way. I don't personally think that it is necessary that to a T you need to standardize reps so perfectly just to be able to track progress. The reality is that whether or not you're touching your chest on something like a bench press, every rep is going to be slightly different. This is just how human motion works. And the reality is we really just don't need a high high high degree of precision and standardization between reps to ensure that we're tracking progress over time. Example, if I don't touch my chest and on the first rep I leave an inch and on the second rep I leave 90% of an inch, well, is the difference between those two reps over time really going to make a difference or is it just going to be obvious that over time I'm clearly getting stronger on the bench press or I'm not. If you don't buy that argument, just look at all the other exercises that you're doing that don't have a perfectly standardized range of motion rep to rep. Think about a triceps push down. Do you always stop at the exact same point? Think about a biceps curl. Do you always allow your elbow to lock or do you stop just shy? Think about a lateral raise. Do you always get up to the exact same point in the rep and move your arm in the exact same path? No, you just strike a rough target and make sure that over time, generally speaking, you're maintaining the quality of that pattern and the control of that pattern as you add load.
