# Why you shake on deadlifts

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/wzd1o2NMCdg>

Why is it that this happens so much when people deadlift? And while you could explain it multiple different ways, I believe there's one key reason that this happens and it can be explained by an analysis of knee biomechanics. So, in the bottom position of the deadlift, the bar is roughly placed over the midfoot. And if we think about where foot pressure is, it goes upward in this direction through the backside of the knee. Meaning that because the application of force is behind the knee, it actually creates a distance like this, which creates a knee bending force. In other words, much like a squat, at the bottom of a deadlift, the quads need to be the thing that actually moves the shin in the backward direction. Otherwise, the shin can't move out of the way. And as the lifter progresses through the lift, that line of force moves closer and closer to in line with the knee. Now, here comes the pivotal moment when the force direction from the floor shifts now in front of the knee. So, now the shin right here is actually behind that force direction. What happens is there's some distance now in front of the knee from the force direction to the knee, which basically means what? That the hamstrings are now the things to be able to actually control the knee position. So, the pivotal moment occurs from the transference between using the quads and the hamstrings. And this is such a make-or-break moment because in that transference, people can get off balance and stop being able to move the bar upward efficiently. So, watch now really closely. The bar moves up, the quads are being used, and now is where the hamstrings start to kick in because the shin has moved far back enough from that bottom position. From where the quads need to be able to pull the knee backward and push the load upward to now where the hamstrings take over, where the quads are still trying to keep the knee still now, but where the hamstrings are trying to extend the hip with that slightly moving knee position. And what I notice a lot of times is as long as people can just get past that phase of toggling, they tend to complete the lift. Now, by the way, this was years ago, and Russ moves this weight basically without any of the shake at all. And you can see him absolutely just smashing pretty much the same weight without any of that toggle. And I think that that happens simply because people just get stronger and more coordinated.
