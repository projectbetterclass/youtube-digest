# Why you only feel shoulders when you train back

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/SFKLuQfStC8>

If you train your lats poorly enough, you'll turn all your lat exercises into rear delt exercises. Even if from the outside it looks like you're pulling nice and tight to your body, you're pulling your elbows to your hips, and you're doing all the things that people say target your lat. But the real key distinction between feeling a lot of your lats and feeling your rear delts and your shoulders in a row is one key thing. And this applies whether it's a row or a pull-down, which is that to target the lats you really want to emphasize and prioritize moving the shoulder blade forward as you stretch and then backward and downward as you contract. Same thing in a pull-down, to really emphasize the lats you want to allow the shoulder blade to shrug upward under control and then shove the shoulder blade downward toward your back during the contraction. If you don't really know how to do this skill, what you're going to end up doing is something like this where it kind of looks like your shoulder blade is stuck or glued to your ribs. And this is a technique that is really going to de-emphasize the lats because as you're pulling the arm back, what's happening is the shoulder is kind of rolling forward. So, if the shoulder's rolling forward, it's almost like the lats are not really changing length nearly as much. Because if you end up just doing this on every pulling motion that you're doing, you're going to end up with a lot more shoulder volume than you are lat volume.
