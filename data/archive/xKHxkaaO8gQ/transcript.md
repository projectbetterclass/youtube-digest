# This is why your calves are small...

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/xKHxkaaO8gQ>

If you've been training your calves consistently, but people still point, laugh, and call you names every time you leave the house in a pair of shorts, this study might explain why. Researchers had participants train one leg with standing calf raises only, and the other with seated calf raises. Everything else in the program was identical. What were the results? Well, after 12 weeks, growth in the soius was about the same in both groups. But in the gastromus, which is the larger, more visible head, it wasn't even close. The standing calf rays grew the lateral head by 12% and the medial head by 9% whereas the seated calf rays produced almost no gains at all. Why such a huge difference? Because unlike the soius, the gastro nemius is a barticular muscle which crosses the knee joint. And in addition to plantar flexion, it also acts as a knee flexor. Meaning when your legs are bent, the muscle is put into a pre-shortened position where it can't generate significant force and doesn't experience much mechanical tension. Bottom line, straight leg calf raise variations should be your go-to choice if you want to maximize gains. And relying on this as your only movement could be the reason why your calves still look like they peaked in elementary school.
