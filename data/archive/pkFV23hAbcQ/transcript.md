# How I deal with 'haters' online

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/pkFV23hAbcQ>

I've been creating content for about 6 years, and I've amassed an audience of over 800,000 people. And the number one mistake that I see so many content creators making is not thinking about haters or hate in what I think is the right way. So, first of all, what is hate? Well, hate is basically a substitute word to use whenever we are offended by something. When we read a comment that strikes a chord, a tender spot inside of us, we label it as hate, and more so we label the person as a hater in order to kind of actually reduce the feeling of discomfort in us. Many people don't realize this, but when they use the term hater, what they're doing is actually putting someone else down to basically say, "Oh, that person's opinion doesn't actually matter. They're just a hater." And when you make this kind of distinction, this kind of change in your language toward another person, what you're actually doing is you're actually preventing yourself from feeling the hurt feelings that that comment, that that person caused in you. And so, rather than taking responsibility for the way that the comment made you feel, for actually trying to understand what about the comment made you feel so offended, so insulted, what people do is they turn it outward. And what happens is people either make, you know, frankly shitty content, or they um never make content in the first place because so much of it is hinging on the response of people who might be offended by their content or who might say something that will hurt their feelings. And so, a lot of really, really awesome, amazing, smart people, a lot of the coaches I work with in particular, are all very hesitant to post on social media because they're worried about this hate, this so-called hate. And really, what people are worried about is encountering their own feelings of triggering as a consequence of other comments. I personally have actually loved being on social media, and it seems many people hate it because of the fact that I actually look forward to when people make these comments that are triggering to me. Because what it means is there's some part of myself that I'm yet to fully understand. And if there is a comment that is triggering in some ways, it means that there's something there that I'm yet to inspect. And so now when I get triggered online, I'm like, "Ooh, that's that's interesting. Like what what in me believes what this person is saying? Where is this person right and where can I actually reflect that back inward to better understand myself?" And so strangely what this does as a content creator, or really honestly as anyone just scrolling on the internet, is it actually turns social media into a sort of machine of like self-discovery, so to speak, or self-understanding, or self-improvement, however you want to put it, where every time you're triggered, you just discover something new about yourself. And what this does is actually allows you to be more creative on social media. Allows you to be more yourself on social media, which just makes it sustainable and actually fun.
