# More volume doesn't equal more gains?

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/dNV97__qpF8>

Ladies and gentlemen, we finally have it. The answer as to whether higher volume is better for muscle growth than lower volume. In this recently released preprint, they took a look at 36 fractional sets versus nine fractional sets. So, four times the volume. And what did they find? No meaningful difference in muscle growth between groups. And what makes this study interesting is that the participants were already experienced with high-intensity training, so these were genuine hard sets. A lot of volume research doesn't tightly control for this, and most people don't train nearly as hard as they think. If effort is sub par, it's no surprise that adding more volume helps. Now, this study used fractional sets, so for example, one set of pull-downs counts as half a set for biceps, but when you add it all up, the low volume group was doing around four to six [music] total sets per muscle per week, while the high volume group was doing about four times that. That said, although this is one of the largest volume studies ever conducted, measuring growth in trained lifters over shorter time frames can still be challenging. So, as TNF mentions, the exact interpretation of the study will vary because people naturally lean toward evidence that lines up with their existing bias. I am admittedly biased toward lower volume training because in my 25 years of experience, it's absolutely clear that most people don't push their sets close to actual failure, which is why they need so many extra sets to compensate. [music] But with the right guidance, once effort is dialed in with proper technique and progression, they tend to see just as good or even better gains doing fewer total sets while spending less time in the gym and feeling less beat up in between sessions. And this study used around the same volume range I used to build my own physique and around the same range as my train wise programs, so the results really don't surprise me. But if you're spending hours cranking out tons of volume, it might be worth asking whether you actually need all that or whether higher quality sets on a smarter program could get you further in less time.
