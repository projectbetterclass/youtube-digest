So, why is it that so many people prefer the incline bench over the flat bench? At a flatter angle, the distance from the load to the shoulder tends to be much greater and much smaller to the elbow. As people move toward higher inclines, the distance to the shoulder actually shrinks and the distance to the elbow increases. If you're doing more of a flat press, the load is simply farther away from your shoulder and as a consequence, there may be more shear force at the shoulder. If you're doing an incline press, even if the load is similar or greater, actually less shear at the shoulder because that distance is smaller. In addition, with the incline, peak resistance tends to be a little bit in front of the body where the shoulder is in a little bit more of a mechanically stable position. Now, if we move more toward a vertical press, we're not really going to be able to have a load that actually stretches the arm behind the body, so that's a different problem. So, the incline press is a really good blend of a flat press and a military press where you can stretch all the pressing muscles significantly, but also put your joints in a position that's a little bit more structurally stable.
