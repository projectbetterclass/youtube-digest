Yet, that's only half the story. Robo taxis transform how we move people, but autonomous trucking can transform how we move everything else. Our entire supply chain depends on trucks efficiently moving freight. And realistically, the trucking revolution might be even more dramatic. Kathy Wood's team at Ark has studied the logistics extensively, and their findings are impressive to say the least. They estimate that autonomous electric trucks could drive the cost of trucking down to around 3 cents per ton mile. Rail, which is the current lowcost option for bolt freight, sits closer to somewhere around 4 cent. If trucks become cheaper than rail, while also offering point-to-point flexibility and 24-hour operation, they will be a no-brainer for every industry. That 1 cent difference between 3 cents and 4 cents might not sound like a big deal in everyday life, but across the scale of the world's economy, it's enormous. That shift could redirect billions upon billions of ton miles into highways. Since electric trucks require less maintenance, experience less downtime, and can be utilized far more efficiently than diesel trucks. The logistics network becomes faster and more resilient. Costs come down dramatically, and the supply chain is changed forever. Here's a better analogy. If robo taxis give people back their time, autonomous trucks give businesses back their margins.
