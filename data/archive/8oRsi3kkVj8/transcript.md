Understanding why requires understanding how rockets currently work and why that's a problem. Every rocket ever built until now has been mostly disposable. Use it once and throw away most of it. Some rockets recover the first stage, but that's partial reusability at best. The second stage burns up, the payload gets discarded, large portions of the vehicle are single use. Building a new rocket for every launch is extraordinarily expensive. Rockets are complex machines with millions of parts. Manufacturing them requires precision engineering. Testing them requires extensive validation. The hardware cost for each launch into tens or hundreds of millions of dollars. That makes space access expensive. Launching satellites costs massive amounts of money. Sending cargo to orbit costs massive amounts. Putting humans in space costs even more. The high costs limits what's possible. Reusability changes everything by allowing the hardware cost to be spread out over many uses. You build the rocket once, you use it many times. The development cost stays high, but it gets spread across dozens or hundreds of launches. The per launch cost drops dramatically because you're just paying for fuel and operations. Airplanes are reusable. You build them once, you fly them thousands of times. The plane can cost tens of millions, hundreds of millions to build, but that cost is spread out over years of service. The per flight cost is mostly fuel, crew, and maintenance. That's why airline tickets cost hundreds of dollars instead of millions. Space access needs the same transformation. Build the rocket once, launch it hundreds of times, drop the per launch price, make space accessible to far more users for far more purposes.
