All right, so a lot of you may be asking the exact same question that I am, which is what is the purpose of this lean forward and backward whenever you're doing some kind of row like this. So, when you're performing a row with a car and you lean forward slightly, what's going to happen is the spine is going to start to flex or round. So, you notice that as he leans forward, the spine goes from what's known as extension here or arching to a more rounded position as he leans. So, the first thing you need to keep in mind is there's a good amount of erector contribution, spinal extensor contribution. So, that's number one. The other thing that changes is as his torso leans forward, you can see that the angle at which his arm is being pulled relative to his to his torso is actually slightly different and it's slightly greater than a horizontal angle. And so, when the upper arm and the shoulder blade gets kind of pulled forward at this diagonal, the lats become proportionally more involved as compared to leaning back. But then, as you can see as he pulls, he's going to do that spine extending, spine arching thing again, creating contraction in his erector muscles. And then, as he leans backward and he pulls the V handle closer to his system, rowing angle is now much more horizontal and maybe even slightly low to high. The cable is now pulling pretty much horizontally relative to his torso. So, what this means is that the recruitment, unlike in the stretch position, will shift upward on his back to the rear delt muscles and then also to the mid upper back muscles that sit between the shoulder blades like the traps and the rhomboids. So, when you're leaning forward and backward, the motion becomes a really big blend of all of the back muscles as compared to a variation where you were just leaning your torso forward and staying there or keeping your torso more upright and staying there.
