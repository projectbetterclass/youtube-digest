§There are many players that are lumped together as AI 
companies, from those spending the money building the data 
centers to the firms benefiting from selling the builders the 
ingredients for these data centers to the companies that are 
leasing these data centers for long periods and immense costs 
(with the LLMs in the lead) to use to create their AI agents, 
models and products.
§I will argue that while all of these groups benefit, at least in 
general, from AI's success at creating large and profitable 
markets, we are reaching a point in the AI disruption, where 
their interests and payoffs will diverge, with implications 
for both the businesses and investors.

§There is one common element that holds them together and that is 
the value of all of the players in the ecosystem ultimately will 
be determined by how big the AI customer group becomes, and 
that is where much of the discussion of total markets and revenues 
converge. 
§As I noted in my earlier posts on AI, if the end market for AI products 
and services is small, either because of its imperfections as a tool or 
replacement for employees, regulatory restrictions or limited reach, the 
entire AI ecosystem (builders, suppliers and product businesses will all 
feel the pain. 
§Conversely, if AI delivers on its promise as a powerful productivity 
tool that works across many businesses, all of them benefit, albeit in 
different forms. In addition, if one of the groups faces growth and 
survival challenges, those pains will spread into the other groups as 
well. 
§That may explain why you see so much circular financing in 
the AI ecosystem, where financially better- positioned and 
stronger players like Nvidia investing in or offer financing 
guarantees to players in the other groups, either to ensure survival 
or to provide an impetus for continued investment spending.

§These businesses also have competing interests, but the 
expenses for one business become revenues for another, and bad 
decisions by one group can create benefit for others. 
§Thus, if AI builders choose to build too much infrastructure, 
too quickly, it will hurt them and their shareholders, but that 
overbuilding will be a bonanza for the AI suppliers, as chips, 
equipment, power and real estate players all benefit from the 
overbuilding. 
§Once built, it is in the AI builders' interests to lease out the 
infrastructure for longer periods than merited, locking in 
their revenue stream, but for the AI product companies, those 
long-term commitments can increase default risk.
§For much of AI's early existence, the commonalities have 
overwhelmed the competing interests, with the groups tending to 
move together on news, mostly about the size of the AI market.
§As the AI disruption moves through the disruption, it is inevitable that 
this will change, with the market differentiating across groups, 
picking winners and losers.

§Public vs Private: Data availability and the requirement that there be a 
market price restricts the sample to publicly traded companies. That 
not only reduces the sample size and creates sampling bias, but it also has 
disparate effects across the different groups. 
§Missed companies and changing sample: Since there is no separate 
accounting categorization for AI-related revenues and expenses, I 
relied on public descriptions of the largest companies operating in each of 
three AI groupings. 
§Not pure plays: Most of the companies in this sample operate in other 
businesses, many of which pre-date their entry into AI. This is especially 
true of the hyperscalers, where Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Microsoft 
have legacy businesses that generate large earnings and stand-alone 
market value
§In multiple groupings: Some of the companies in this sample can take issue 
with the grouping that I have included them or operate in more than one 
grouping. Meta, Alphabet and Microsoft, in addition to be major investors 
in AI cap ex, are working on their own versions of LLM (Muse for Meta, 
Gemini for Alphabet and Co-Pilot for Microsoft) to enter the product 
market.

§I am seldom certain about anything in markets, but I am close 
to certain that there is a big market delusion in AI, I think 
that AI, because of its reach and potential, almost certainly has 
led to over investment, by AI builders, over commitments to 
compute power by LLM companies and over pricing, by the 
market, at least in the aggregate. 
§The correction, when it comes, will cause pain, but the pain 
will be distributed unevenly, and for those who believe that AI 
has potential but have found the companies in the space over 
priced, it will provide opportunities. 
§Before you decide that this (the over investment, over pricing 
and subsequent correction)  is a problem, it is worth noting 
that this phenomenon is a feature, not a bug, that is 
associated with uncertainty and big change, and that it is, in 
spite of the costs of the cleanup, is a pre-requisite for change.

§Uncertainty about correction (when and what form): Even if you are right that 
investor and business overconfidence has lead to overreach, you need the 
market to come to agree with you, and it is difficult, if not impossible, to try 
to predict when in will happen, and what form it will take. 
§Catalysts for correction:The catalysts that trigger the correction can sometimes 
be big news stories, but more often, it is a collection of small cuts that 
eventually leads to market adjustment. The big market delusion is ultimately 
built on a behavioral characteristic (over confidence) and the correction has 
as much to do with investor psychology, as with financial fundamentals. 
§Acting in the fact of uncertainty:If you are an investor of long standing in one 
of AI’s winners, use each correction phase to sell a portion of your shares.

§As I noted in an earlier post, IPOs are priced, and after not 
hearing talk of annualized revenue run rates for a few weeks, there 
was a leak that Anthropic's ARR for October hit $80 billion, which 
was touted as both good news (by some) and bad news by others. 
§The truth is that pricing is a mood and momentum game, and it is 
undeniable that the mood has darkened for not just AI but the 
market in the last two weeks. If you throw in bankers who are 
terrified of over pricing a trillion-dollar plus offering and 
picking up the pieces, the pressures to lower the offer pricing 
only get stronger.
§On the valuation front, it is not market mood that matters but 
events that have changed the fundamentals Just to give you a 
measure of how sensitive the Anthropic valuation is to these 
changes, increasing how much Anthropic will have to invest to add 
compute power in future years (by lowering the sales to cap ratio 
from 3 to 2.5) and increasing the drag on margins from regulation 
(from 5% to 7.5%) reduces the estimated equity value to $1.25 
trillion, even if you leave the target revenues at a trillion dollars.

§I hope that we stop talking about "the AI trade", as a catch all for AI 
companies, and start differentiating between the different groups "AI 
builder" or the "AI Supplier" or the "AI product trade, for two reasons.
§The first is that as the AI business starts to take form, there will be developments 
that are good for one group and bad for others; the election results clearly 
affected AI suppliers more negatively than it did AI builders. 
§The second is that when you have crisis developments, the market may lose 
its head and mark down all AI companies indiscriminately, offering you the 
chance to buy those that you believe should not have been marked down.
§I also think that it is time that we hold off on making overly broad 
assessments of the AI business that reflect more our emotions and 
priors than the facts on the ground. 
§Thus, if you start with the presumption that the entire phenomenon is a 
"bubble”, your mind is already made up and no AI company, no matter what 
group it belongs to, will be worth its price. 
§At the other extreme, if you are so dazzled by the technology that you see this 
as a sure win, you are setting yourself for disappointment, because not only are 
there no sure wins in equity markets, but the pathway to AI nirvana will be come 
with challenges that test your faith.
