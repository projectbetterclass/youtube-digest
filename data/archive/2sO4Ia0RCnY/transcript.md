# Framing negative feedback

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2sO4Ia0RCnY>

When you need to provide negative feedback, the most important tool to utilize is framing. You got to frame the conversation. You don't just go into the conversation. Let's say for example, you're giving somebody negative feedback on the way they communicate. Hey Dan, I'm going to be giving you a lot of feedback on how you can improve. Dan, please I want you to know this feedback comes from a place of love, okay? Some of the words you hear, you might go, "Ah, it's going to hurt me a little bit, but please I want you to know it comes from a place of love. It comes from a place of me wanting you to improve and you to amplify the best parts of who you are." Otherwise, if I don't frame it, then I immediately go into things like, "Oh, Dan, I'm noticing right now that you're using your body in a non-functional way. Your body language is too small and you're falling victim to the prayer position. That is not effective nor is it influential." Now, if I didn't frame that and I just went straight into that, you'd sit there and go, "Oh, damn it, I have terrible hand gestures. I'm using my voice poorly. Ah, I suck." Whereas, when you know it comes from a place of love and you know that everything I say has the purpose of amplifying the best parts of you, you receive it differently. You You listen to it and you're like, "Oh, yeah, I'm feeling inspired. Yeah, I should work on that." Often, when people give negative feedback, they don't frame. So, I frame it with love, support, kindness, compassion, and motivation, and then I go into the feedback.
