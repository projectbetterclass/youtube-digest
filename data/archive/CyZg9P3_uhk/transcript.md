So, now let's cover some common mistakes made with pec training in general and how you can address those. Mistake number one is letting the arms go too far away from the torso in the stretch position of any pec movement. The pecs attach and wrap around the rib cage, which means that whenever we're doing a pec-based movement, we want our arms to be tighter to the ribs. If the arms are not tight to the ribs, the pecs cannot maximally stretch around the rib cage. Another problem with wider grip pressing is that it also exposes the shoulder to greater potential risk for injury. Pecs are a huge muscle group that can contribute a lot to pressing and to wrapping the arm around the body, and when we disadvantage the pecs, we put tax on other smaller muscles that are not apt to handle those kinds of loads. Oftentimes, most of the pec tears that you'll see with heavy bench presses occur with a super wide grip at the bottom portion of the movement when the pec is supposed to be most advantaged, but isn't. Mistake number two is only sticking to the barbell bench press. The barbell bench press can be a great movement for developing strength specific to that movement itself, but it's not a great exercise for hypertrophy because the range of motion is inherently limited by the constraints of the bar. The exercises I've previously showed, like the dumbbell bench and the cable press, are much, much better options because not only do they encompass more range of motion for the target tissue, but they also allow for more degrees of freedom that allow you to manipulate your arm path. Mistake number three is not training frequently enough. A lot of times, people want to grow their pecs, but aren't willing to put in two to three days per week into the gym to target all of the different specific fibers. I would recommend starting out with two different days that you train pec specifically, and make sure that you hit all three divisions on each of those days individually.
