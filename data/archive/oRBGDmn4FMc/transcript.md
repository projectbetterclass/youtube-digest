# Setting a baseline timer for 2-3min is a good idea but you can always just rest longer if needed

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/oRBGDmn4FMc>

Tell me why I'm still seeing people at the gym who are setting the timer and the minute that timer hits that >> He does have a lot of points that I do agree with in his other videos, but this is one of those ones that I would disagree with. I do see the point that he's trying to make though. He's essentially saying that you shouldn't be using the same amount of rest time for each exercise, but saying not to set timers whatsoever just because of that I would disagree with because you can set different times on the rest timer. I have training teams for six different programs and of course I'm using a variety of exercises in those programs. For those exercises I write the sets, the reps, the tempo, and the rest times. Now for those exercises I vary the rest times depending on the type of exercise and about how long I think it's going to take to recover. So I typically write a range for that exercise. It might be something like two to three minutes. It could be something like three to five minutes. And I often have people ask me if they can rest a little bit shorter or they can rest a little bit longer. And what I typically tell them is if you were still breathing hard before your next set, then yeah, rest another 30 seconds, rest another minute until your heart rate goes back down. You do not want to be huffing and puffing going into your next set as that is going to affect the stimulus of the next set. So I would agree with him in saying that you should not be rushing into your next set regardless of whether your timer went off or not, but I do think timers are very useful especially since I see most people rushing into their next set when they're not using a timer. So having that timer to at least set a baseline minimum to rest and then reassess at the end of that timer is pretty smart.
