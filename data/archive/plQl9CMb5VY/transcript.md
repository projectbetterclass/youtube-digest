# You definitely don’t have to train like this to maximize gains 😅

A unilateral exercise is one of the best ways to bring up a lagging muscle. However, if you're a beginner lifter, you really don't need to rely as heavily on these isolation unilateral movements compared to a more advanced lifter. This is because a beginner lifter has yet to develop the muscle fibers that a more advanced lifter has already maxed out. So, while a unilateral or isolation exercise is still going to be beneficial for a beginner lifter, they're much better off doing a compound exercise, which is going to be much more efficient as it's going to stimulate more overall growth. In contrast, advanced lifters who've already developed the fibers that are activated earlier on benefit more from exercises that maximize motor unit recruitment like isolation and unilateral exercises, especially if they've hit a plateau for that muscle. That way they can ensure that they're targeting the fibers that still have room to grow. So, if you're still within your first year or a couple years of training, there's nothing wrong with including some unilateral or isolation exercises, but I would make sure that the majority of your movements are bilateral compound movements. That way you can maximize the time that you're spending in the gym and be as efficient as possible.
