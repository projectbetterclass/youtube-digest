# Some of the most common mistakes I see with tricep pushdowns

This is an exaggerated version of what I don't want to see when somebody's performing their tricep pushdowns. Do not let your upper arm drift up, keep it locked in place at your sides. If you like to step back and hinge when you're doing your tricep extensions, this isn't a big deal, but if you feel like you're being pulled forward, then I would suggest doing this. Walk it forward, that way the cable is staying closer to perpendicular with the floor and this should feel much better. And if you're using a longer attachment that causes the weight to re-rack between your reps, then you can try kneeling in front of the cables, which is going to resolve that and help with your stability. And if you find that straight bar or easy bar pushdowns give you some elbow discomfort, then you can try the long rope that I was showing in the previous clip or something like a cross body extension here. And for a setup tip that's going to make your life a bit easier, don't set the cables too high. Set the cables right above where you're ending your eccentric for the rep. Lastly, there is no need to spam this exercise. Doing this twice a week with two to three days in between, doing two to three sets a session is going to be more than enough to grow some big triceps. And as for the rep range, you really can't go wrong working anywhere between five to 12 reps based on your preference.
