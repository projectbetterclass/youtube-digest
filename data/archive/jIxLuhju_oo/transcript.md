# Why don’t people train hard?

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/jIxLuhju_oo>

Why don't people train hard? The obvious answer is that hard training is physically painful. So, the real question is what makes it that some people can train through that pain and others just simply can't. Well, to me it's mostly about the relationship that someone has with the pain. Do you relate to the pain like it's something you want to get rid of or do you treat it like it's exactly what you're looking to get out of your workout and what you're looking for? The most muscular people that I know have a relationship with the pain that is strangely enjoyable, right? They literally will look forward to it. And the people who never seem to get themselves to train hard ever are the ones who literally will flinch at the thought of doing a hard set of any exercise. So, now the question really becomes well, how do you go from being the person who's scared of the pain to the person who is genuinely looking forward to it every time you train? I believe that there are two crucial and kind of oversimplified stages here. So, the first stage is to create a genuine sense of curiosity about the pain itself. Right? When does it actually start to hurt? What does it actually feel like? What happens inside of the muscle itself literally when you begin a set versus when you start to fatigue? And number two, just notice, notice how quickly your mind wants to escape the experiences of these sensations as they intensify and simply return your attention to what your body is actually experiencing, be it the pain itself or literally any other physical experience of the exercise. Attempting to escape the pain, attempting to escape the experience is literally the same as making it worse. So, when people try to reduce the pain, they're actually just inviting in the very thing that they're trying to avoid, right? The body will either shift away from loading the muscle, which often times makes the set longer and in fact more grueling, and or you'll just stop the set ensuring that you won't actually develop the skills that you would need to otherwise make hard lifting more enjoyable down the line.
