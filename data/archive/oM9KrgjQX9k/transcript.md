Demis then talks about AI that can model the world itself, and that opens a whole new world, quite literally. World models help AI understand how things change over time, which is key for building agents that can complete tasks on their own. This is super important for the development and improved performance of robotics, video prediction, and decision-making tasks. A world model is basically an AI learning how the world works. It builds an internal map of cause and effect. It learns that if you move something, it changes position. If you pour water, it flows. If you turn a wheel, it rotates. This seems simple to us, but models today often don't understand these basics. They only know patterns in language. World models are different. They help AI move from guessing the next word to understanding events. Real tasks are not one-step problems. You don't clean the kitchen with one instruction. You don't plan a trip with one line. You don't run a business with a single command. Tasks have stages, deadlines, steps, and updates. A world model can hold all of that together. Another useful part of world models is predictive ability. These models can imagine what will probably happen next. For example, if you show a model someone pushing a box, it can predict the box will slide. If you show it a person reaching for a cup, it predicts they will grab it. Prediction is the foundation of planning. Without prediction, an agent is guessing. With prediction, it can organize tasks in a smart order. Let's take Genie, the interactive video system Demis mentioned as example. Besides it being fun, the real importance is that the scene stays stable. That means the model understands objects, positions, and movement well enough to keep everything consistent. That is early evidence of understanding rules, not just images. When you combine world models with agents, things start to change. An agent with a world model can remember what it's doing, figure out what should happen next, and adjust plans when things change. This is very different from today's agents. In everyday life, this could show up in a simple way first. An agent could handle a school project, manage a weekly schedule, keep track of bills, organize photos, or manage to-do lists that update automatically. It won't be perfect, but it will follow the logic of tasks more closely than AI today. In workplaces, early agents might manage customer requests, prepare reports, track orders, or schedule meetings across time zones.
