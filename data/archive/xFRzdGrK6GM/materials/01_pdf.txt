The	Background
¨ Britain	joined	the	European	Common	Market	(the	
precursor	to	the	EU)	in	1975	in	a	referendum,	where	
two-thirds	of	the	voters	voted	yes.
¨ While	it	has	continued	to	be	part	of	the	EU,	it	chose	
not	to	adopt	the	Euro	as	a	currency	by	opting	out	of	
the	Maastricht	Treaty	in	1992.
¨ In	2014,	David	Cameron,	the	prime	minister ,	
promised	as	a	lead-in	to	the	general	elections	to	put	
the	question	of	whether	the	UK	should	stay	in	the	
EU	for	a	referendum.
3
Remain	versus	Leave
¨ The	Remain	camp:	The	Remain	camp	was	lead	by	
David	Cameron	and	much	of	the	establishment,	but	
it	split	the	parties	in	terms	of	backing.	
¨ The	Leave	camp:	The	Leave	camp’s	most	visible	
campaigners	were	two	conservatives,	Boris	Johnson	
and	Michael	Gove,	but	its	most	ardent	promoter	was	
Nigel	Farage,	the	leader	of	the	UKIP .

2.	The	Emptiness	of	Expertise
1. The	Specialist	Syndrome:	Expertise	has	become	
narrower	and	more	specialized	than	ever	before,	
leading	to	prognosticators	who	are	incapable	of	seeing	
the	big	picture.	
2. Mistakes	are	public:	While	economic	experts	have	
always	had	a	mixed	track	record	on	forecasting,	their	
mistakes	now	are	not	only	more	visible	but	also	more	
public	than	ever	before.	
3. The	Global	Economy:	The	mistakes	experts	make	have	
become	bigger	and	more	common	as	we	have	
globalized,	partly	because	the	interconnections	
between	economies	means	there	are	far	more	
uncontrollable	variables	than	in	the	past.
14
3.	Narrative	versus	Numbers
• Ironically,	stories	have	become	more	important	in	a	
world	where	numbers	have	become	more	plentiful.	
• In	the	Brexit	debate,	it	seemed	to	me	that	the	Leave	
side	had	the	more	compelling	 narrative	(of	a	return	
to	an	an	old	Britain	that	some	voters	found	
appealing)	and	while	the	Remain	side	argued	that	
this	narrative	was	not	plausible	in	today's	world,	its	
counter	consisted	mostly	of	numbers	(the	costs	that	
Britain	would	face	from	Brexit).
15
4.	The	Governance	Question
¨ The	Political	Governance	 Divide:	 While	some	 (mostly	 on	the	
winning	 side)	 celebrate	 the	 power	 of	free	 will,	 those	 who	 have	
never	 trusted	people	 to	make	reasoned	 judgments	 on	their	futures	
view	 the	vote	 as	vindication	 of	their	 fears.	
¨ The	Corporate	Governance	 Divide:	 Divide	 between	 those	who	
believe	 that	shareholders,	 as	the	owners	 of	public	corporations,	
should	control	outcomes,	 at	one	end,	and	those	who	argue	that	
incumbent	 managers	 and/or	insiders	 are	more	knowledgeable	
about	businesses	 and	should	therefore	be	allowed	 to	operate	
unencumbered,	 at	the	other.	
¨ Democracy	 can	disappoint:	 In	a	democracy ,	 you	will	get	outcomes	
you	 do	not	like	 and	throwing	 a	tantrum	 (as	some	 in	the	 Remain	
camp	are	doing	right	now)	or	threatening	 to	move	 (to	Canada	 or	
Switzerland)	 are	not	grown-up	 responses.
16
The	End	Game
¨ The	Crisis	test:	Acting	in	the	midst	of	a	crisis	has	
never	worked	for	me.	The	actions	that	I	take	when	I	
am	panicked	are	more	likely	to	hurt	than	help	me.
¨ The	Auto	Adjustment:	Markets	adjust	your	portfolio	
to	the	new	reality.	If	you	do	more,	you	may	over	
compensate.
¨ The	Dangers	of	Bottom	Fishing:	Blind	contrarianism	
versus	a	search	for	market	mistakes.
¨ Canaries	in	the	Coal	Mine: If	there	is	a	systemic	 risk	
problem,	it	will	show	up	in	the	banking	sector .
