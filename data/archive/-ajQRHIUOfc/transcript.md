# Why the incline press isn't my first choice for a beginner/intermediate lifter

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/-ajQRHIUOfc>

The incline press would not be my first choice for a beginner to intermediate lifter, and here's why. The mid to low pec is going to do much more for your chest size and the overall aesthetics of your pecs compared to the upper chest. The upper chest makes up a much smaller portion of the pecs, and it's not going to give you that that hang. A lot of people are way too focused on trying to build this shelf, which a lot of people are also not even lean enough to see to begin with. So, it's a waste of time in my opinion for a beginner to solely be focusing on incline pressing. Instead, I would stick to a flat or 15° bench press that can be barbell, that can be dumbbell, that can be machine pressing, whatever, but I would make your main press a flat press. If you wanted to make your main press an incline press, then I would suggest pairing that with some kind of flat fly, like a pec deck fly, for example. And here's the thing that a lot of people don't understand. Even if you're only doing a flat press or a flat fly, you are still going to be heavily involving the upper pec in that exercise. So, you're still going to be stimulating the upper pecs really well throughout the week. However, with an incline press biasing the upper pecs, you're going to be getting less of a mid to low pec stimulus out of that exercise. And I don't think that's that helpful for somebody who has not built up a solid chest to begin with. So, if you haven't been seeing the chest progress that you want to see and have been mainly incline pressing, try making that switch.
