# The real reason you're not progressing

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Xi6oM6Ty9TI>

The reason you're not making progress right now probably isn't because of the program you're doing. It's probably not because of how many sets you're doing, how many reps you're doing, or even what split you're running. The number one reason you're probably not making progress is because you skipped a stage in your lifting. When you were a beginner, progress came easily. You didn't have to think much about really anything. You just pushed and pulled weight, and your muscles grew, and you got stronger. But because progress comes so easy early on, most people are never actually required to develop the most important skill in lifting, which is the ability to perform higheffort single repetitions. All of the progress that you will or won't see, all of the pain and injury that you will or won't experience is downstream of the single reps that you do on any given exercise. All of the questions about programming that you have, how much volume you should do, how many reps in any set, what split you should run are all downstream of the single repetition that you do. Many people waste years in their lifting journeys looking for broad answers to programming questions when the reality of the answer is often right in front of them in their own experience that they're not paying attention to. Three things that determine the quality of any rep. Number one, is this aligned with what I'm actually trying to do? You need to know anatomy and physics to understand this. Number two, am I actually controlling the movement? Which more specifically means, am I resisting the force of the exercise throughout the range of motion that I'm choosing to use? And number three, can I maintain a focus on number one and number two as fatigue sets in? Can I keep the goal the goal? And can I adjust the range of motion? And can I maintain control over the resistance as I get tired, as I get into the painful reps of a set and as the repetition slows down or becomes shorter in its range. Every question that you have about programming is based on this skill. So if you haven't developed this skill, pick one exercise, just one exercise for every muscle group and get really, really, really good at the single repetition that you're doing. You will probably be shocked at how much progress you make just by honing in the skill.
