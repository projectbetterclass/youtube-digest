# BAD Lateral Raises

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/0-8UJFjDn-Q>

Have you ever been told that the right way to do a side lateral raise to hit the middle delts to act like you're pouring a pitcher of water with your thumb pointing down and your pinky held high? If so, please stop. Pouring the pitchers at the top of the lateral raise places your shoulder into internal rotation from an elevated position. This can cause irritation and inflammation of the tendons of the rotator cuff due to a lack of clear joint space in the position leading to shoulder impingement. Rotating your arm inwardly as you do lateral raises is not inherently a bad thing. And rotating your arm this way actually puts the middle delts more in line with the resistance so that the middle delts get proportionately more stimulus. And this fear-mongering around shoulder impingement is related to people saying that these two spaces touching one another is bad. But there's no evidence actually to support that this kind of motion is bad or dangerous in any way. And guess what? Even if these things do start to come closer together, your shoulder blade will simply start to tip upwards so that they don't actually touch. And again, this whole shoulder impingement thing is based on a mechanism that hasn't actually been shown to be true in practical scenarios. So if rotating your shoulder inwardly during lateral raises increases the stimulus to the middle delt for you, then keep doing it.
