Ab training really doesn't have to be that complicated, and it doesn't have to look like you're doing some kind of circus act. Here's a quick guide on all the abs. Rectus abdominis, also known as the six-pack, runs from the bottom of the sternum and the ribs to the top of the pelvis. When it contracts, it brings these two points together. So, a very simple way to train them is just to use a standard cable crunch where you're just bending and extending straight forward and backward. The external obliques run all along the side of the ribs and attach to the rectus abdominis and the hip. Because they run in this direction, they can create a lot of different motions. Mainly rotation away from that side and also side bending. So to train them, we can use a rotational exercise either with a cable or just with body weight. Or we can do crunches where we're rotated away from the working side. The internal obliques kind of run in an opposite direction. So instead of running high to low, they kind of run more like this. Because they run across this way, they can rotate the ribs to the same side of the body and in addition close the gap on that side of the body too. So internal obliques can get trained with rotation toward the working side as well as side bending.
