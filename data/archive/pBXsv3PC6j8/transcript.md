# The Truth About "Good Form"

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/pBXsv3PC6j8>

If you've been training consistently with good form, but you still look like a barista whose heaviest lift is the milk frother, this might be the reason why. It's because you think good form looks like this when it actually looks more like this. Staying in control of the weight is definitely important, but lifting so strictly and so slowly to where it basically looks like you're lifting underwater, that's probably hurting your gains rather than helping them. For the best growth stimulus, the lifting phase of the rep should be performed as hard and fast as possible. And if that includes a slight bit of natural body movement, that's fine as long as you're not sacrificing technique just to ego lift. A controlled lowering phase is important to keep your reps consistent, but you don't need to go super slow there either. 2 to 4 seconds is more than enough. The bottom line is to find a healthy balance between maintaining solid form while also exerting with maximum effort.
