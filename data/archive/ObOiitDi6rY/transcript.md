Leg press is for people who just want the easy way out. A squat pattern is a must for quads. It's actually incredible to me how many people think like this, including coaches out there, because I've seen a lot of programs where there are three, four different squat exercises within one workout that essentially all train the same thing. But since they're different variations or it's on a different machine, they can't tell that it's basically training the same muscles. Getting back to the topic from Sean's video though, believe it or not, a leg press is actually even more effective in a lot of cases than a barbell squat when it comes to your leg growth. Bro lifters are going to go absolutely insane hearing that, but this is true because a leg press is going to involve less bracing and it's going to be even more stable compared to a barbell squat. So, it's going to be even easier for the person to take [music] the target tissue close to failure. A lot of people might fail that barbell squat due to how much they have to brace, the demands on their core, the cardiovascular demand. Compared to the leg press, they're able to push much harder without those other things interfering with their ability to push the muscle to failure. The leg press is one of the best tools for growing your legs and it can be used in a ton of different ways. If somebody has a lower back issue, then it's a great option for them. Or if you have, say, stiff leg deadlift coming up that week and you want to make sure your erectors are fresh for that and you don't want them to be toasted from all the barbell squats that you were doing, then you could do leg press instead. If you don't understand things like this, then you probably shouldn't be writing your own programs and you definitely shouldn't be coaching.
