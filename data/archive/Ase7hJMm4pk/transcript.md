First, I was wondering whether or not this could remotely compared to the seated adduction machine. And I tried it and it did. And for those of you who are curious, in principle, all that's happening is your hand is basically just being smooshed against your leg. So your hand is acting as a proxy for the pad as in the case of a seated adduction. A couple things I would recommend for this motion. Number one, if you want to make it a little bit heavier in the stretched position, scoot the bench a little bit farther forward as I'm showing here. And if you want to make it harder toward the shortened position where your knees are close to touching, just move the bench a little bit backwards so that you're positioned roughly directly between the pulleys. Another thing is at some point it might get hard to hold on to the handles simply because of grip fatigue related issues. So, make sure if that's the case, you use some kind of strap. Overall, if I had the seated adduction machine, I would still choose that. But this is a great option for many of you who are looking to train adduction directly, who don't have access to that
