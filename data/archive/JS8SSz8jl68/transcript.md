# Big muscles ≠ good advice

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/JS8SSz8jl68>

Why do we often think that more muscular people give better gym advice? And what if the very thing that makes us trust their advice is also what makes us vulnerable to bad advice? Well, here's one perspective. The more muscular someone is, the more likely it is, to be honest, that they've had to solve many of the same problems that less muscular people struggle with. But, there's an obvious problem here, right? Because the problems that someone else has had to solve and the extent of those problems themselves may actually bear no resemblance to your particular situation. And even if they do, whether someone can translate that into advice that actually lands and helps you is a totally open question. And in the same way that those who are born into wealthy families can effectively fake financial wisdom, genetically gifted outliers and/or drug users can hide poor advice behind their physiques to basically convince you that they understand something that they actually don't. So, here's a more useful question, I think, to ask yourself. What makes me want to rely so heavily on the advice of others before I've actually experimented for myself first? What is it that makes it that I don't ask myself what I think of something before handing authority over to someone else? Because the reality is that anyone you admire probably evolved because they learned how to listen to themselves deeply before anyone else. So, the real question is, how can you start to trust yourself?
