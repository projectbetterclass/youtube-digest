# Do you do this for quads?

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/0QkTrywsA50>

If you want quads that look like they can kick holes through redwoods, then don't miss these tips. Instead of putting your leg extensions last like the middle child of a family, try putting them before your main squat movement. While a quad by a squat movement is excellent for building some big legs, they don't train the rectus femoris, which is the middle quad muscle, whereas a leg extension does. A squat movement is also more fatiguing than a leg extension and will cause a larger drop in performance in subsequent lifts in comparison. Since leg extensions are an isolation exercise, you'll also get better recruitment of the target tissue in comparison to a compound movement. Even more so if you're doing it unilaterally as shown here in comparison to bilaterally, but bilaterally is still a great choice, especially if you're a beginner lifter or want to be more time efficient. Just make sure to rest at least a couple minutes before your next set, that way the other leg still gets a good training stimulus. Another benefit of doing leg extensions before your squats is that it helps with warming up your knees and may help with alleviating discomfort during your squats. This alone could help with improving your performance on your squats, therefore causing even better quad growth. All this being said, if training your glutes or your adductor magnus is more of a priority for you over your quads or you're a new lifter, then it may still make sense to put your squats first over the leg extensions. This is because a squat movement is going to do a better job of training more overall muscles. Hope this helps and as always, make sure to like and follow for more.
