# This does train the glutes, but it's more hamstring volume since the knee is straightened

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/1mBth1WH-UY>

Holding the weight here versus here is going to feel easier in comparison because when you're holding the weight out here, the moment arm is much longer. A simple way to understand and feel this is if you're holding a weight here and then trying to hold a weight here, which one are you going to be able to do longer? Same concept. And it's for that same reason when I'm doing my weighted crunches that I will hold the weight over my head rather than over my chest. That way I don't have to hold quite as much weight. I also wanted to address the people that I saw in the comments saying that this was a lower back exercise because she was not rounding her lower back when performing it, therefore it was not going to be glutes. I would recommend that she slows down her eccentric, that way she can work into a more lengthened position for the hamstrings and glutes, and that way she can maintain that range of motion throughout the set. I have on this in the past, but you do not have to round your spine to make this a glute exercise. So long as you are setting it up properly and maintaining a neutral spine and are focused on hinging, then you are going to be hitting the hamstrings and glutes well.
