So, what actually happens if you only do two sets per workout? Is it enough to make gains or is it just maintenance? Well, we actually have research on this and the answer probably isn't what you think. This recent study showed that just one set done twice per week was enough to produce significant gains in trained lifters. Another meta-analysis found that as little as four weekly sets per muscle can stimulate measurable hypertrophy and we have multiple other papers showing the same thing. Very low volumes absolutely can build muscle effectively. What most people don't realize is that the first one to two sets of the workout provide the strongest muscle building stimulus and after that you get sharp diminishing returns while fatigue continues climbing. Now, if you're only doing two sets once a week, yeah, that's probably far from ideal. But on something like full body three times a week or upper lower four times a week, that adds up to four to six total weekly sets per muscle. Here's the key though. This only really works if you're actually training hard. If you're pumping out three to four RIR sets to mild discomfort like most high volume guys do, two sets likely would be maintenance at best. But if you're truly dialing in and pushing those sets very close to failure with proper execution, two sets done multiple times a week can produce great results.
