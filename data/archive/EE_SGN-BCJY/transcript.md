If you're not taking a multivitamin and you're on the fence about it, this video is for you. I honestly think that most people should be taking one considering that there are minimal risks, if any, and the potential [music] benefits are high, especially if your micronutrient intake is lacking, which it is in a ton of populations. Reports have shown inadequacies in vitamins A, C, D, E, calcium, choline, magnesium, iron, zinc, especially older populations and potassium. I've seen a lot of people who hate on multis try to use this review by Kim and colleagues to dismiss the use of a multi entirely since it concluded that using a multivitamin did not improve cardiovascular outcomes in gen pop. However, I really don't think that should be used as conclusive evidence against the multi. I mean, in the review, to be considered a multi, it only had to include four vitamin and/or mineral ingredients, and only five of the 18 trials even specified the ingredients. That being said, most well-formulated full spectrum multis are going to include much more than that. And not to mention, that is only one review. Blumberg and colleagues found that the use of a multivitamin compared with food alone was associated with a lower prevalence of inadequacy for most 15 nutrients including vitamin A, C, E, and zinc. Another study by Bird and colleagues found that supplement non-users had the highest risk of any deficiency, 40% compared to users of full spectrum multivitamins at 14%. So, clearly, multis can make a difference. And here's the thing, while getting your nutrition from food is ideal, most people are not eating a diverse enough diet to optimize micronutrient intake, especially people who are following a certain specified diet or in a deficit where food volume and diversity is lower.
