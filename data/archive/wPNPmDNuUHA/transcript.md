# The average person could make great progress just doing 1-3 sets

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/wPNPmDNuUHA>

When I first started training 16 years ago, I was 14 years old. Three to five sets per exercise was the norm. You would not find people doing less than that typically if they were a serious lifter. And you may be wondering why I have Terrance in the background behind me here, and the reason being is his caption and what he tried recently. He dropped everything to a single working set during an 8-week recovery period, and not only did he maintain his size, but he added strength to almost every single lift. Let that sink in. We're talking about one of the best bodybuilders in the world, and he was able to maintain size and add strength with only one set per exercise over an 8-week period. And before you guys say that wouldn't work for a natural lifter, I've been programming like this for years within my training teams. One to three sets per exercise, thousands of natural lifters in there, and they're all making phenomenal progress with a more moderate approach to their volume compared to a higher volume approach. And if that's still not enough to convince you that you can make good progress doing lower volume, Jeff Nippard essentially cut his workouts in half for 100 days, going from three to four sets to one to two sets. And he did this while cutting, mind you. So, imagine if he was at maintenance or in a surplus. This was his DEXA scan result from day 30 to day 100. This is likely within the margin of error, so we can at least say that he maintained muscle while losing fat on lower volume. Not only that, but he was able to maintain his strength and hit PRs on a lot of his lifts. If you haven't been making the best progress lately or your joints are always hurting, instead of trying to add more sets to make more gains, maybe just try cutting back a bit or join my training teams and see how much better the progress is.
