# Can You Do Breathing Exercises with a Deviated Septum?

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/5AO6szlhG3E>

One of a colleague of mine, who I had taught a meditation practice to many years ago, recently told me that they actually had to have their deviated septum surgically corrected. Can you do these practices with a deviated septum? First thing to understand is deviated septum is not binary. There's degrees of deviation. So, you can have a slightly deviated septum, or you can have a septum that is so deviated that it basically blocks like one of your sinuses off. Second interesting thing is depending on when your septum got deviated, it can change the like the muscular architecture, and you actually some people need to do a breathing rehabilitation. So, you have to retrain your breathing muscles to be able to breathe properly because when you have a deviated septum, basically since you have no capacity to breathe on one side, the muscles and tissue in that adapt accordingly. So, what kind of breathing practices you can can you do? So, first thing is either do some amount of nadi shodhi or not. I'm sure that someone somewhere will claim that doing enough alternate nostril breathing will correct your deviated septum. I would not believe that. Next thing is you can just do lots of practices that are just don't involve one nostril. So, bellows breathing is a good one, fire breath is a good one, cooling breath is another good one. These are These are practices that basically you use both of your nostrils. And the last thing is there is a more advanced practice than alternate nostril breathing that is a mental alternate nostril breathing where you literally just visualize the air. You don't have to block anything physically. You visualize something coming in and going to the tip of your forehead. And then when you breathe out, you visualize it going out, and then you breathe in and out, but your nose is completely open. So, that's actually like an advanced version of alternate nostril breathing that will still have more spiritual as opposed to physiologic benefits. But people are like, "What do I do?" These are your options. Get your deviated septum fixed surgically, do practices that don't involve one nostril at a time, or do the mental equivalent of alternate nostril breathing.
