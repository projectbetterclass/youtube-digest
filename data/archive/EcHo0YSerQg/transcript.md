Here's why trying to lift correctly might actually be getting in the way of you developing good technique. So, imagine you're doing an exercise, say it's a deadlift, and you've been told that doing it correctly is lifting with a straight back. What if you have relatively short arms and you go down to grab the bar and your back rounds? All of a sudden, you go into a panic and you think to yourself, "Uh, why can't I do it with a straight back? Everyone else does it with a straight back." So, you get down on yourself and you try to basically contort your body to abide by some sort of arbitrary standard that culture has made up and imposed on you, and you get frustrated. And maybe you go through so many iterations of trying to do that technique that you never actually find the one that's right for you. Alternative example, imagine you're doing a bench press and you've been told by culture, "Oh, you must retract your shoulder blades and depress your shoulder blades to do an effective bench press." But the problem is that when you do that, your shoulder bothers you, or maybe you just can't really connect with your chest. And as soon as you actually start to allow for some shoulder blade movement more freely, all of a sudden, your shoulder pain maybe goes away and your chest lights up with contraction. So, which is correct? Well, obviously, the one that is working for you. But if you have in your head that correct is outside of you, then you never actually begin to learn how to run your own experiments in the gym and therefore how to find the techniques that are actually good for you and your body in this very moment. And that, how you create a truly unique and individualized program for yourself, is when you've experimented with so many different exercises, so many different muscle groups, that you've created something for yourself that no one possibly could have created for you. So, what exercises and techniques are you still stuck believing are correct? And what are some ways that you could go about experimenting with them?
