Quantitative easing is actually where the Federal Reserve increases the money supply to encourage investing and borrowing by buying bonds on the open market. So, when you hear big news channels talking about tapering, what they're talking about is the Federal Reserve slowing down how many bonds they're buying month over month until they're no longer buying any bonds at all. Once that happens, the Fed can begin raising the federal funds rate, which is also called the overnight lending rate. Currently, that rate is close to zero. The federal funds rate is the interest rate that banks charge each other when they borrow money, which in turn determines the short-term interest rates that banks and other lenders charge companies and individuals to borrow that money from them.
