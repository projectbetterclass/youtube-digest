# How I recommend setting up pec deck

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/1jXOVqS_NMU>

I recently came across a video of a kid that tore his bicep and no, he was not doing anything like curls, preacher curls, where you see a lot of those tears occur. He was using the pec deck. And I've said for years not to start the pec deck with an extended elbow position like so, as it's going to place a lot of unnecessary load on the bicep tendon. I'm not going to post a video because he's been given a hard enough time by a lot of other creators, and clearly it was not an issue for him for many sessions, so he didn't think it was an issue up until the point where his bicep tendon just could not handle the load anymore. So this is a good learning lesson for us, and I hope he has a speedy recovery. This is how I recommend setting up your pec deck, especially if you want to use heavier loads. You're going to grab one side with both arms and then twist around, grab the other side. I like to lock my hands at the start there, that way I can set up. And then you're going to initiate with bent elbows, sending the upper arms back. As you're coming back, you're allowing the scapula to retract, but you are not trying to keep it pinned back in place. You're also keeping the elbows pointed back in the direction that the machine is going. And while you can do a fly or pec deck with your arms fully extended, the elbow locked out, I really don't see any reason to load the bicep like that when the goal of this movement is to work the pecs. Your pecs are going to be able to load more than your biceps are going to be able to handle, so I don't see any reason to limit yourself by having your arm extended like that. Try not to let these videos of freak accidents scare you guys from going heavy so long as you're using good load management and allowing for proper recovery.
