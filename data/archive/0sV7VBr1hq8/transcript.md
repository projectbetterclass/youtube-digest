If your back does not have that pop to it when you're hitting your front relax, your front double bicep, your front lat spread, then this is probably your issue. Whenever we're talking back development, it seems like people always fixate on growing the lower lats. But if you want that pop in your lats, what's actually going to make the biggest difference, especially from the front, is going to be your upper lats. So, if you don't have good upper lats, then your back is simply never going to pop from these front poses. Let's go over some of my favorite exercises to grow the upper lats. One of my favorites is the lat pullaround. You're basically having your bicep touch your nose as you're coming across your torso. This is going to lengthen the lats and get you a great stimulus for not only the upper lats, but also the lower lats as well. This would be my choice for a more lengthen exercise. For a more short position exercise, training the muscle in its more contracted position, I really like a seated cable row, slightly leaning back. That way, you're working through lower degrees of shoulder elevation with a neutral grip. For this exercise, you're thinking about driving the shoulder down and scraping the ground with your elbow as you're pulling back in line with the side of your torso. If your gym has a chest supported version of a neutral grip row that you can do, then that would also be an excellent option. Maybe you already do an exercise like this, but if you haven't been putting it first within your program at least a couple times a week, that should really help your back development.
