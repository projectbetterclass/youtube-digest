# How does leg length impact barbell squatting?

why is it so much easier for people with shorter femur to stay upright while squatting anyone with relatively shorter femurs can keep the line of force of the bar directly over their midfoot fairly easily while still keeping their torso upright watch what happens when we increase the length of the femur when the length of the femur increases we have to move the Torso back with the hips but now look what happens to the line of force of the barbell it actually moves behind the foot so in order to not fall backward we actually need to lean the Torso forward to move the line of force over the midfoot again and this is why people with generally longer femurs end up having to hinge over so much more so that they can keep the line of force directly balance over their midfoot so they don't fall backward and they don't fall forward keep in mind though that this is ultimately about the relationship between the tibia the femur and the Torso and not about the absolute length of any one segment so if we just increase the length of the tibia look where the line of force moves it actually moves forward so now we actually have an ability to move our torso more upright so ultimately the kind of squat that you do will be determined by the relative length between your segments and not the absolute length of any one segment
