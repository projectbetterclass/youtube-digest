# Which row should you do?

What's the difference between doing rows like this, where you stay upright, versus where you allow yourself to lean forward and backward like this? When you do normal rows where your chest is just remaining upright like this, you'll be primarily targeting the upper portions of the lats, the ones that run more horizontal, as well as the rear delt and a little bit of the upper back like the rhomboids and the traps. Your erectors will also stabilize your spine from rounding forward, but they don't have to do that much work. When you lean forward and you round your back like this, what you're essentially doing at this portion of the range is you're turning this into more of a vertical movement. In other words, you're changing it from a more horizontal pull to a more vertical pull because you're just tipping your torso forward. In this position, you'll involve more of the lats overall. When your arm stretches forward in this direction, your lower lats are going to be the ones to hold your arm onto your body. And then as you pull backward and you arch your back out of that position, your spinal erectors are going to have to do a lot of the work to pull you back up. And then by the time you get to the top position, the muscle recruitment is the same as in the first one. So overall, leaning forward and backwards simply allows you to target more of the total musculature of the back to include the lower lats as well as more spinal erectors. Whereas keeping your back static will involve more of the upper lats and proportionately the upper back. Overall, one of these exercises isn't better than the other. You just have to figure out what you're actually trying to target and then work backward from there. If you're trying to target more of the upper lats and the upper back, I would stick to the variation where your torso isn't moving. But if you want to target basically every muscle on the back side of the body, you can tip your body forward and backward because that will allow you to distribute the load through more total tissues.
