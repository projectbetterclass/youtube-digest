# What a set to failure looks like (14 years old)

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Q5E2Ncm0u7k>

[music] [music] [music] >> So this guy's only 14 years old and I actually love seeing this because I started training right around the same age with that same type of intensity. A lot of people will tell you that novices shouldn't do low volume high intensity because apparently it takes years to learn how to train hard, but as someone who's been doing this for over two decades, I can tell you that's complete BS. I've seen countless advanced lifters who claim their training to failure when they easily still have three reps left in the tank and I've seen complete beginners who train like absolute psychopaths. Experience usually helps, but training hard isn't about how long you've been in the gym. It's mostly about having the right mindset and the right instructions. If you have both, then pushing most exercises within a rep or so of failure is perfectly realistic in a pretty short time frame, often literally a matter of weeks. Sure, the more complex movements like squats and hinges will usually take more time, but you also don't even have to go this hard by taking every set to zero RIR and you'll probably find a better overall balance between stimulus and recovery by only doing those absolute all out grinder reps more sparingly. [music] But don't let anyone tell you that you can't train hard purely based on your experience level. You're not a delicate flower just because you're a novice and if you actually believe that, you'll probably hold back and limit your progress without even realizing it.
