Being more upright isn't necessarily wrong, but when your torso is parallel to the floor, gravity is pulling directly against your scapular retractors. This forces your mid traps and romboids to work much harder. The only way to make a traditional higher torso angle row hit the same is by compensating with more weight. But pound-for-pound, penlay rows are simply more challenging. I'm going to respectfully disagree with a number of things here. Let's talk about why. Hinging over more so that your torso is parallel to the floor, will not be better for your scapular retractors, aka your mid traps and your rhomboids primarily. This is because retraction is actually a slightly backward and upward motion, at least when you're trying to target those upper back muscles, which means that when you're slightly more upright, the loading is actually a little bit more specific to the mid traps and the rhomboids. When you bend over so that your torso is parallel to the floor, the lats actually gain more leverage to contribute to the motion. Specifically, the upper lats, which prevent the shoulder from getting pulled off of the body in a forward direction. So staying a little bit more upright will actually be more specific to the upper back tissues if that's part of your goal. And staying more upright will actually make it easier on your spine and your spinal erector muscles so that you don't have to work very hard to stabilize the motion. Hinging all the way over so that your torso is parallel to the floor will be less specific to the upper back because the motion will include a greater amount of lat recruitment and also a greater amount of erector recruitment because the bar is now farther away from your spine. Neither one of these motions is necessarily better than the other. You just really have to hone in specifically on what your goals are. So if your goals are to target mostly just the upper back, I would actually stay more upright.
