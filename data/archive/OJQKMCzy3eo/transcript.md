# Do you do Kelso shrugs? While I like this mechanical drop set I prefer them as straight sets.

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/OJQKMCzy3eo>

Most people do their T-bar rows like this, but when you think about it, all of this motion is just your biceps. And this motion is your rear delts. Your biceps and your rear delts are way weaker than your back. That's why anytime you go to failure on a row, it's usually not your back that gives out. Let me prove that to you. Go to failure the next time you do T-bar rows. I'll bet you any money you could get at least another two or three mid-back squeezes with locked arms. That should tell you that your biceps hit failure, your rear delts hit failure, but your mid-back didn't. Those mid-back squeezes are called Kelso shrugs. This is actually a technique that I like to implement within my training, especially if I'm short on time and I want to hit a bit more traps and rhomboids. However, whether you personally are going to be able to perform Kelso shrugs after you finish your rows is going to depend on your individual strength for your traps, rhomboids, rear delts, your elbow flexors, as well as your form. If you are failing a row due to your elbow flexors, your biceps, [music] that's typically going to be due to a form issue. You're probably using too narrow of a grip and perhaps even thinking about pulling the hands in towards your arm, which in turn is going to involve more of the elbow flexor, so you may need to take a slightly wider grip. If your rear delts are limiting you, then you could be pulling way past your body, which is going to involve much more of your delts, which is not necessarily a bad thing, so just depends on what you want to focus on. If you want a well-rounded upper back row, this is the form I would suggest using. Notice my arm path and how far back I'm pulling up. I'm allowing for full protraction and retraction of the scapula. And if you are someone who can do Kelso shrugs after their T-bar rows, even with good form, then I think it's still a worthwhile thing to do and allows you to get a bit more trap and rhomboid work in with less time in the gym. If you do add them in and you're doing multiple sets of rows, then I would save them for the very last set of rows that you're doing.
