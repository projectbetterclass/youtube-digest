# How I structure my added cardio

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/CMBpCWQKyK0>

Do not do any sort of intense cardio before you go and lift. If you're trying to maximize the amount of muscle growth that you're going to see from your workouts, then it's a really bad idea. If it's light cardio, that's totally fine. 10-15 minute walk to help you with warming up if it's cold outside when you get to the gym, totally fine. But if it's any sort of intense cardio, jogging, running, etc., don't do it. All that is going to do is fatigue you and lessen the stimulus from your workout. If you want to do some sort of cardio after you lift, then that's fine. That would be a better place to put it. If I am planning some sort of intense cardio, then ideally the best place to put it would be on your rest days rather than a lift day. When I was running more frequently and I was doing a 3-4 day lifting program, then I would just run on the rest days. Or if I had to run on the same day as a lifting day, then I would put it on an upper body day rather than a lower body day. There are also certain types of cardio that are going to be more fatiguing, for example, running due to the landing phase and the eccentric loading. Compare that to something like cycling, swimming, rowing, etc. Those are going to be less fatiguing options and are less likely to impact your workouts. Hope this helps and as always, make sure to like and follow for more optimal training tips.
