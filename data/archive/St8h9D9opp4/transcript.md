Start at the heaviest and then have to get lighter over time. >> This is genuinely one of the dumbest ways to approach your training. And unfortunately, it's also one of the most common. All it does is put you in this weird middle ground zone where the earlier sets are too light to stimulate meaningful growth, but they're still challenging enough to pre- fatigue you on the way to the main sets that actually matter. If you're capable of lifting the 70s, you want to go into that set as fresh as possible so you can execute it at full capacity and get maximum stimulus rather than wasting energy on a bunch of useless junk sets. I'm not saying don't warm up, but a proper warm-up shouldn't be fatiguing whatsoever. I typically go with around 50% of my working weight for around six to eight reps, 75% for two or three reps, and then a single rep at 90 to 100%. Simple as that. And this is also where a lot of high volume guys kind of make me laugh because they'll talk about doing 15 to 20 sets per muscle, like it's a badge of honor, when a good chunk of it were just these useless, lowintensity pyramid sets. So, in reality, they end up doing a similar number of true muscle building sets compared to low volume lifters, but just less effective versions of them because of all the extra fatigue they created leading up to them.
