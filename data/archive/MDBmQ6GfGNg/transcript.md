# Are your "gains" just an illusion?

Huge mistake. Oh, I'm going to change my routine every four or six weeks. huh? If your squats work for you, you need to be doing squats. Why are you going to change it to one-legged cable Your body doesn't know what it's exercise it's doing. It just knows how hard it's working and if it's working beyond its normal capacity, then it has to adapt to that. If you change your workout every few weeks, you actually will see faster progress, just not in the way that you think. See, muscle size and strength are very tightly correlated. A bigger muscle is a stronger muscle and tracking your strength gains is the best gauge you have for hypertrophy. And when you swap in a new set of exercises, you usually will see your weight and reps climb up at a faster rate. But here's the catch, strength is also neurological. Those early gains on a new exercise are largely from your nervous system just getting better at the movement and using your existing muscle more efficiently. So, on paper it looks like you've hit a new spurt of muscle growth even though that's usually not what's actually happening. That's why you want to keep your program consistent over a set period rather than constantly switching things around. If you're steadily adding weight to the bar after a few weeks and months on the same set of lifts, that's a much clearer sign you're experiencing actual hypertrophy rather than just learning a new movement pattern.
