Investors remember Intel's dominance in CPUs, giving way to declining growth and margin compression. They assume Nvidia faces similar dynamics where competitors emerge, custom chips fragment the market, and growth inevitably slows. But Nvidia's business model differs fundamentally from historical semiconductor companies because AI deployments create compounding demand rather than replacement cycles. Every company deploying AI needs compute for training and inference. Every autonomous vehicle needs AI chips. Every robot needs edge computing. Every data center needs accelerated computing for modern workloads. This demand doesn't disappear during economic downturns the way consumer PC sales collapse. The comparison to mobile phone adoption and the iPhone dismissal by Nokia reveals we're much earlier into AI deployment than the market pricing suggests.
