Stop expecting to get stronger on your lifts every single week. It's not realistic. And if you've been stuck at the same weight and reps for several sessions in a row, you just need to trust the process and give it more time. That's the standard line most fitness coaches will tell you. But that advice kind of sucks because if you're still a novice to intermediate lifter and you're in a focused muscle building phase, then yeah, you should be making measurable progress on most of your lifts most of the time. If you're not, something in your program is off, and time alone isn't going to magically fix it. Remember, hypertrophy causes strength. The reason your body adds that new tissue is to make the muscle stronger. So, if the previous workout provided a sufficient growth stimulus and your diet and recovery were on point afterwards, you will come back to the gym stronger in some form. That could mean lifting slightly more weight, but it could also mean performing an extra rep or two with the same weight, or even half a rep, or even the same weight in reps, but with faster bar speed or stricter technique. Progress isn't always dramatic, but it should be noticeable and consistent. Of course, you'll have off days because of external factors like diet, stress, or even a lack of motivation.
