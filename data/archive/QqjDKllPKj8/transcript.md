Chest is one of the most overemphasized muscle groups, second maybe only to biceps, [music] and you don't need to hammer it from six different angles in every session. All divisions of the pecs perform horizontal adduction as their primary function, aka bringing your upper arm across your body. The upper pecs perform shoulder flexion as well, but they make up a smaller portion of the overall chest and are still being hit hard at normal flat angles. So as long as you're doing some type of flat press or fly plus an incline variation across the week as a whole, you're basically good to go. [music] For example, on the train-wise three-day full body split, you'll do one chest exercise per session, [music] and on the three-to-four-day upper-lower split, you'll do two chest exercises per session. If you're training hard with good technique and a focus on progression over time, that's easily enough for complete chest development.
