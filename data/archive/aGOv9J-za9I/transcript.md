And here's why. In my opinion, muscle contraction is actually a response that your brain generates based on the position that your body is in and the forces that your body has to deal with. In other words, contraction occurs as a consequence of the forces of the external world. And that's what you go to the gym to do. You go to the gym to impose force on your body so that specific muscles can respond and grow and get stronger. And if you take this approach of I'm going to learn how to really squeeze the muscle on my own first. What you are teaching the body to do is to create resistance against itself. Let me give you a quick example. If I wanted to train my triceps and contract my triceps, the only way to contract my triceps without any load is to squeeze it as hard as I can. What that does is it actually activates muscles on the front side of the elbow like the biceps and the other elbow flexors. Why? Well, because if there's no resistance against you extending or straightening your elbow, you have to create it on your own. In the same way, if you make a muscle like this and you squeeze your biceps, you'll notice if you poke your triceps, they're actually kind of hard. This is because in order to contract your biceps, your triceps need to pull on the other side of the elbow to give the biceps something to pull against. But when you go to the gym to lift weights, you can simply just put a cable in your hand and start doing curls or start doing triceps extensions. But my contention is that we should learn how to be in our bodies by choosing the right exercises for the target muscle group.
