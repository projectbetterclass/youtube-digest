# I’m curious, how many of you guys regularly do isometrics within your training?

People hating don't know isometrics are technically better than average sets. Is that actually true though? First off, you can grow muscle by doing solely isometrics because they do generate a high degree of mechanical tension. The biggest flaw with isometrics compared to regular lifting is your inability to track progression because with isometrics all you really have is the time that you're doing the isometric for compared to in the gym you're able to log your reps and weight and see how you're progressing over time. Aside from helping you maintain muscle mass when you have limited equipment here are some other things that I like using isometrics for. If you've gotten to a point where you're maxing out a machine then you can start including isometrics that way you can continue progressing using that same machine. Isometrics can also be very useful during rehab if dynamic training tends to cause a lot of pain and discomfort. For example, when I was recovering from my pec tear surgery I was using isometrics to help with getting back into the gym. Isometrics can also cause joint specific strength adaptations so for example, if somebody finds that they are very weak at say the bottom of a squat or their bench press then you can include an isometric in that position. Using the bottom of a squat as an example again, this is a bit different from a max effort isometric but I find that if somebody has discomfort in that position I like including a pause there which is going to help with building tolerance at that joint angle. Doesn't have to be a super long pause but I found that doing a 1 to 3 second pause in that position can help a lot.
