Before we start breaking down this exercise, can I just say I am incredibly tired of this trend right here? My glute gains, my bicep gains, whatever gains doubled, tripled, quadrupled ever since I started doing this, and it's always an intermediate to advanced lifter who's been lifting for years, gained a bunch of muscle doing other stuff, they tried something new, they saw something novel online, and then they made a video like this to get engagement. Let's just stop that. Getting to the exercise, let's talk about what this is doing. When you lean more forward during hip abduction, you're going to bias more of the glute max versus the medius. So, the larger the crease in your hip is, the more you're going to bias the glute max. If your goal is training the glute max via its function of abduction unilaterally, then this could be a good option for you. However, just using this machine normally is pretty much going to do that already. If you did want a unilateral variation that trains the glute medius a bit better, then try this out. Scoot your butt forward in the seat, lean back, and then you can do it unilaterally like this. The hip is less flexed, you're better biasing the glute medius. Don't have time to do it unilaterally, then try this out. Same concept, you're leaning back, but doing it with both legs.
