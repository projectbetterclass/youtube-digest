What's the difference between doing a front squat and doing a back squat? Well, when you're doing a back squat, you have to tip everything forward so you don't fall backward. When you tip forward, the tendency is more to hinge, and so the back squat tends to be more hip dominant unless you have something like a heel elevation. When you put the weight in front of you like this, your whole body can actually fall backward, so you can stay more upright with the front squat. But the problem with the front squat is that whatever you have in front of you, a barbell, a dumbbell, requires you to not drop something straight forward. So, a lot of the limitation can actually be on the trunk and the muscles [snorts] of the trunk to try to stabilize the body. So, even though you're more upright in a front squat, it might end up being actually harder for your spine muscles than your legs. So, I would recommend a back squat, and specifically a high bar back squat with a heel elevation to load the legs the most, and then to adjust the technique, whether it's hips backward or knees forward, for your goal. But just remember, none of these motions are good or bad in absolute. Really just depends on what you're trying to do.
