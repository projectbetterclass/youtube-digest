People are always asking me, "Ben, why do you like to set up your cable lateral raises around nip height?" Thus, the nip height cable lateral. Well, two reasons primarily. Number one, because the cable is kind of pulling at a slightly upward angle in the bottom position, you see, relative to my arm, the chances that I and clients who I use this with shrug up against the load is very, very minimal. Number one. Number two, as you can see, the drop-off in the top position, i.e., when I get to the top of the range, is humongous. Okay, so look at the cable and the line of the cable, and look at the angle in the bottom versus the top. In the top, it's getting almost close to parallel with my upper arm, which basically means that there's very, very little resistance on the shoulder. This is super convenient and super helpful because we're much weaker toward the top position. Whereas, when you do it at hip height, the drop-off isn't so steep, and so your last reps end up being a bunch of partials, which isn't necessarily bad, but I just kind of prefer to do less partials at the end.
