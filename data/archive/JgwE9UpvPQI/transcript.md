# What is a "speed bump" exercise?

<https://www.youtube.com/shorts/JgwE9UpvPQI>

I'd like to coin a new term for a certain kind of exercise, and it's going to be called a speed bump exercise. So, why are they called speed bump exercises? Well, because they force you to take your foot off the gas. Basically, any exercise that you can't really load substantially. Speed bump exercises are perceived as mystical, magical in a million different ways, and largely just because of the fact that they reduce total stress of the system. These include, but are not limited to exercises that are looked at as corrective in some way, stability exercises, or just any variation that basically prevents you from actually using a substantial amount of load and stressing your body in any significant way. On the one hand, these things can be kind of useful because they prevent people from actually pushing themselves super super hard when maybe they shouldn't or help them recover from injuries when other exercises might actually load them significantly more. and that may help recovery. But overall, I think it's wise for everyone to get to the point where they're able to manage the amount of work, effort, and load, and volume in their program that they're doing so that they don't have to include speed bump exercises. Remember that no exercise is inherently harmful. And I think this concept of the speed bump exercise is actually quite helpful because people seem to think very categorically about, oh, this kind of exercise is a stability thing. It's over here. And this kind of a thing is is like a lifting thing. It's over here. No, these are all just exercises, us interacting with the physics of the world on a spectrum of high magnitude to low magnitude, high effort to low effort. And it's important that we understand that no exercises are magical. Certain exercises just provide more or less stress to the same or different areas.
