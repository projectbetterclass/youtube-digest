## [Why You Never Feel Your Back | Biomechanics Explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JSQaAQndlyg)

**Ben Yanes** · ✅ Watch in full
> Provides a clear, reproducible framework for analyzing exercise biomechanics and gives specific, actionable cues (arm path relative to torso, scapular movement) that directly improve exercise selection and technique.

**Key points**
- Back muscles feel less activated in certain pulldown variations due to two factors: the direction of force relative to the body, and the body's position relative to that force.
- Arm position matters: when arms flare outward and away from the torso, the stimulus moves away from lats; effective lat engagement requires pulling the arm toward the torso.
- Scapular movement is key: exercises that move the shoulder girdle as a unit (elevation/depression, protraction/retraction) create greater lat activation than those emphasizing only upper arm movement.
- The demonstrated pulldown prioritizes rear delts and teres major over lats because of torso arch, arm width, and minimal scapular movement—stimulus is distributed across multiple muscles rather than concentrated on lats.
- Process for analyzing exercises: identify the major force direction, understand the body's position relative to that force, then determine which muscles can resist that load from that position.

**Takeaway:** Lat activation depends less on the exercise type and more on whether the arm moves toward the torso and the scapula moves as a unit through a large range; most people neglect scapular movement and instead only move their upper arm, missing the opportunity to fully engage the lats.

## [Don't Do THIS On Leg Extensions...](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/RFaKieqL5No)

**Ben Yanes** · ✅ Watch in full
> Quick, practical biomechanics correction with clear demonstration of why a common cue fails and how to avoid it.

**Key points**
- Don't use hip flexion (pulling legs upward) on leg extensions—the machine pad only moves horizontally forward/backward, not vertically
- When you pull upward against the fixed pad, you fight the machine and must drastically reduce weight (demonstrated at 60 lbs vs. original weight)
- Hip flexion cue creates excessive rectus femoris engagement but doesn't load the knee properly or move the actual load
- Leg extensions are designed to load the knee in the specific arc the shin naturally moves—front to back, not up and down
- Pulling upward is ~90° off from the machine's actual motion vector, making it biomechanically counterproductive

**Takeaway:** Don't cue hip flexion (knee-to-hand pulling) on leg extensions; the machine pad moves horizontally only, and this technique forces you to use far less weight while creating misleading muscle sensation rather than proper loading.
