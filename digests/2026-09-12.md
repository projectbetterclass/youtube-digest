## [WHY YOU'RE TRAPPED IN LIFE](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Z_Oj7v_WB60)

**The Diary Of A CEO** · 🟡 Skim
> The core insight—that unacknowledged trade-offs cause paralysis—is clear from this clip and captured in the key points; no need to watch the full version.

**Key points**
- The video references Buridan's donkey thought experiment: an animal paralyzed between two equally desired resources (water and food) ends up dying of hunger and thirst.
- Applies the parable to human decision-making: people often trap themselves by wanting contradictory things simultaneously (e.g., a creative life without rejection, running a marathon without tiredness).
- Claims the word 'but' is the hidden mechanism—we voice competing desires internally but don't acknowledge the trade-off explicitly.
- Suggests this internal conflict and denial of trade-offs leads to paralysis and inaction.

**Takeaway:** 
Many people stay stuck in life because they want mutually exclusive things but refuse to acknowledge the trade-offs, hiding behind the word 'but' instead of choosing one path over another.


**🔍 Reality check:** 
The video frames indecision as a universal human trap, but doesn't explore that some "buts" are often solvable (with time, creativity, or compromise) rather than truly binary. It also assumes explicit acknowledgment of trade-offs automatically resolves paralysis, which isn't always true—some conflicts remain genuinely difficult even when named. The thought experiment is illustrative rather than a precise model of human psychology, and real decision-making involves factors beyond binary choice (priority, sequencing, partial satisfaction, etc.).
