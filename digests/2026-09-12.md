## [How the "Skilled Money" Picks Stocks](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/MF5CZt2PgHs)

**Felix & Friends (Goat Academy)** · 🟡 Skim
> The core insight (rules + discipline matter more than headlines) is valuable but brief; the key points capture the lesson without requiring the full video.

**Key points**
- Felix Prehn ran a live poll asking followers where they would sell Google stock; after all answered, he revealed Berkshire Hathaway had nearly doubled its stake in Alphabet, making it one of Berkshire's three largest holdings.
- When asked if this news changed their answer, everyone said yes — demonstrating how quickly external information can override pre-set trading rules.
- The lesson: having clear exit rules means little if you abandon them when headlines or other investors' actions surface; automation and discipline are required to stick to your plan.
- Prehn emphasizes he ignores who buys or sells a stock and relies only on technical exit triggers that he does not override based on news or sentiment.

**Takeaway:** The video illustrates a behavioral finance problem: most investors abandon their own trading rules the moment a compelling news story emerges, showing why having a written plan and automated exits is critical.

**🔍 Reality check:** The poll is a clever illustration but applies to a small, self-selected audience; it doesn't prove that *all* investors or that more sophisticated traders react the same way. Also, Berkshire's buying decision could be material new information (not just 'noise'), and ignoring all external data in favor of pure technical signals alone is itself a trading philosophy that carries real risks. The video frames narrative-following as always wrong, but sometimes the right move is to *change* your thesis based on new facts."

## [Look confident + sound confident = Feel confident](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/J3BFof2monw)

**Vinh Giang** · 🟡 Skim
> The core idea is sound and actionable, but the 60-second format only captures a brief example; the full mechanism and evidence for why this works aren't fully explored.

**Key points**
- Behavior shapes belief, not the reverse: act your way into confidence rather than thinking your way there.
- Practicing confident behaviors (volume, gestures) feels fake at first but becomes genuine through repetition.
- External feedback from others reinforces the new behavior, creating a feedback loop that shifts internal belief over time.
- The process is gradual—each repetition and positive response moves the needle toward internalized confidence.

**Takeaway:** Shifting external behaviors—like speaking louder and using larger gestures—can gradually change your beliefs about yourself through repeated practice and social feedback.

**🔍 Reality check:** This reflects a real psychological principle (behavioral change can precede belief change), but the video doesn't acknowledge that success depends heavily on context and consistency—faking confidence awkwardly in front of skeptics may backfire, and individual differences in how people internalize feedback matter. Also, this is a motivational frame, not established neuroscience; the causal chain he describes is plausible but presented as inevitable rather than probabilistic.

## [What Breakups ACTUALLY Do To Men](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-cf78hK8Esc)

**HealthyGamerGG** · ✅ Watch in full
> Grounded framework (equifinality, self-concept) cuts through contradictory breakup advice; cites data on recovery time and attachment; actionable techniques; avoids one-size-fits-all prescriptions.

**Key points**
- No-contact advice is impractical for ~50% of people with shared responsibilities; equifinality means multiple coping strategies can work if they rebuild self-concept
- Early, long relationships hit hardest after breakup because identity becomes fused with the partner; men show higher medical risk (takatsubo cardiomyopathy) when identity loss is severe
- Median recovery time is 4.18 years; anxious attachment prolongs recovery, avoidant attachment speeds it; being dumped (vs. dumping) also slows recovery
- Balance activation (grief, emotions, conversations) with deactivation (exercise, friends, hobbies) to avoid rumination; excessive emotional avoidance increases PTSD-like outcomes
- Rebuild identity by (1) living in service to what you truly care about, not compromises, and (2) broadening self-concept—exploring who else you can become, not just filling the relationship void

**Takeaway:** Recovery from breakups centers on rebuilding your independent self-concept, not just emotional processing or avoidance; research suggests median recovery takes 4+ years, and the specific strategy (problem-solving, emotional activation, friendships, new interests) matters less than whether it restores your sense of identity separate from the relationship.

The 4.18-year median is from one study and has a "wide range"; individual recovery varies enormously. The advice is helpful but presented with confidence; some recommendations (e.g., rebound relationships helping recovery) are supported by data but carry social/emotional risks not fully explored. Heavy promotional framing (multiple links to paid coaching, memberships, books) suggests creator has financial interest in viewer engagement; no indication this is sponsored, but context matters. Measured overall—not overstated—but the emphasis on 'self-concept' as the unifying solution may oversimplify complex grief and loss.

## [“I’m Fully Booked, Why Am I Not Profitable?”](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/ey7B2gAjBi4)

**Alex Hormozi** · 🟡 Skim
> Core insight is solid and specific to insurance-dependent service businesses, but the video is a short clip and the advice (hybrid pricing strategy) is straightforward enough to capture here.

**Key points**
- Physical therapy clinic owner with $1.2M revenue but only ~10% profit margin ($120K), despite being fully booked.
- Root cause: fully insurance-based model means no price control; revenue scales only by cutting costs (Walmart model) or expanding locations.
- Scaling via pure cost-cutting leads to diminishing returns: at $5M revenue, profit might only be $500K–$750K across five locations, negating growth benefits.
- Two paths: (1) Stay lean and accept low absolute profit growth, or (2) Shift to non-insurance leads to recapture pricing power.
- Hybrid bridge strategy: educate patients on premium care options outside insurance coverage to create a higher-margin revenue stream without abandoning insurance base.

**Takeaway:** Fully booked doesn't equal profitable; in insurance-only models, the only lever is cost-cutting, which scales poorly. Introducing non-insurance offerings or premium care options can restore margin without losing the base business.

**🔍 Reality check:** Hormozi frames this as a structural problem and solution, which is largely sound for insurance models; however, the hybrid strategy assumes (a) patients have disposable income and will choose premium care, (b) clinicians have capacity to upsell without cannibalizing insurance volume, and (c) no regulatory/payer blowback. Real execution depends heavily on market, staff buy-in, and local reimbursement rules—not a guaranteed fix. Also, the profit projection at $5M ($500K–$750K) is illustrative, not a formula."
