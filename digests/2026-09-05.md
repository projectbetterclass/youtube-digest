## [Right Person, Wrong Seat | BUILD](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I0WoAw2Wn4o)

**Leila Hormozi** · ✅ Watch in full
> Practical frameworks for a hard but unavoidable founder/CEO problem; specific scenarios and concrete solutions (role redesign, dotted-line reporting, seasonal role swaps) immediately applicable to scaling teams.

**Key points**
- Right person, wrong seat: Evaluate skills vs. current role; consider role redesign, reallocation of duties, or shifting job focus rather than assuming the person is the problem.
- Right person, wrong season: Acknowledge that life circumstances change (family, health, fatigue); offer less demanding roles if available, or respectfully part ways without viewing departure as failure.
- Right person, wrong boss: Misalignment isn't personal; use dotted-line reporting, mentorship, or alternative leadership relationships to retain good people when chemistry is missing with their direct manager.
- Business seasons matter: Early startup chaos vs. mature company structure; some people thrive in wartime/competition and others in peacetime/stability—know which fits each team member and adapt roles if possible.
- Coaching and effort alone cannot fix misalignment; strategic thinking about role fit, job design, and life season prevents wasted energy and protects business performance.
- Firing is sometimes necessary after exhausting options, but most underperformance problems are placement or context issues, not character issues.

**Takeaway:** Before firing or giving up on a team member, diagnose whether they're actually the wrong person or simply misplaced—wrong role, wrong season of life, or wrong reporting relationship. Strategic replacement often recovers value that coaching alone cannot.

**🔍 Reality check:** Hormozi's own position shift (CEO to executive chairwoman) during filming illustrates her point honestly, but the framework assumes healthy founder-leadership flexibility that not all businesses have. The suggestion to redesign roles, create EA positions, or hire duplicative roles to retain talent works best at scale with capital; cash-constrained early-stage companies may not have that luxury. Also, the video conflates personal loyalty/character with performance capability—these can coexist while a role fit still fails, but the diagnostics require honest individual-to-role assessment that emotional attachment often clouds. No mention of what happens when someone genuinely can't perform and has no better role: the advice to "think strategically" works better with humility about limits."

## [How To Fix Pain | Biomechanics Academy Podcast #5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yji5goqrjAs)

**Ben Yanes** · ✅ Watch in full
> Substantive reframe of pain management backed by lived examples; the methodical experimental approach and permission to abandon false certainty is genuinely useful for anyone managing recurring discomfort in or out of training.

**Key points**
- Pain is a neutral signal of sensitivity that requires investigation and curiosity, not fear or immediate removal; structural abnormalities do not reliably predict pain and vice versa.
- Stop trying to determine the 'root cause' of pain—certainty about causation narrows your solution set and is epistemologically impossible even with imaging; focus instead on observable patterns and experimentation.
- Adjust variables within training and daily life systematically: joint position, range of motion, load, tempo, intention, implement, resistance profile, effort, exercise order, frequency, and volume to find what feels good.
- Use immediate, local adjustments in exercise context (arm path, grip width, range, tempo) for acute pain; address broader life variables (sleep, sitting posture, screen height, commute) for chronic pain.
- Adopt an experimental mindset: observe without judgment, test interventions, avoid reinforcing false certainty when something works, and trust the body's wisdom rather than imposing mechanical fixes.

**Takeaway:** 
Pain is a non-diagnostic signal; fixing it requires dropping the need to know 'why' and instead systematically experimenting with movement, loading, and lifestyle variables until you find what feels good. The framework matters more than any single technique.


**🔍 Reality check:** Yanes acknowledges he is not a medical professional and this is not medical advice—important qualifier. The framework is strong on psychology and pragmatism but light on underlying physiology; research citations are sparse and the claim that "structural disruption does not equal pain" is accurate but can obscure cases where imaging *does* reveal serious pathology (fractures, severe disc herniations with neurological signs). His dual case studies (bicep curls, lower back) are anecdotal; success with experimentation doesn't mean a serious condition won't require medical referral. The 'don't make assumptions' message is sound, but for chronic or severe pain, working with a skilled clinician while adopting this exploratory mindset is not either/or. The sales links to his paid community appear at the end, a minor but present monetization angle.

**Materials:** Yanes links to his free Biomechanics Mini Course and paid Biomechanics Academy courses in Skool. No peer-reviewed sources or external research links provided in the transcript; the content is entirely premise-and-anecdote-based, which limits external verification but does not reduce its practical utility for immediate troubleshooting.
