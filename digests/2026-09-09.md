## [How to (actually) load the side glutes](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/IQYBTLH0hhI)

**Ben Yanes** · 🟡 Skim
> Practical coaching cue for a specific exercise variation; technique advice is solid but limited in scope and doesn't require watching the full short.

**Key points**
- Standard leg kickouts (including Instagram-angle versions) move the femur rather than the pelvis, limiting glute loading and stability.
- A better alternative is a standing hip shift: shift weight onto one leg while holding a heavy weight on the opposite side, then drive that leg downward against the load.
- This pelvis-over-femur movement pattern emphasizes the stretch position on side glutes and allows much heavier loading than kickouts.
- The hip shift is more intuitive to learn and teach, and avoids common issues with leg kickouts like poor load progression and instability.

**Takeaway:** 
For effective side glute training, use a standing hip shift with contralateral loading (heavy weight in the non-working hand) rather than leg kickouts, because it prioritizes pelvis-over-femur movement and enables better progression and stability.


**🔍 Reality check:** The video makes a reasonable biomechanical point about load orientation, but offers no evidence (EMG, studies, before/after data) that this method is objectively superior to kickouts for hypertrophy or strength. "All these muscles will light up" is subjective feel, not measured outcome. Many people see good results from varied approaches; this is one coach's preference, not universal truth.

## [Most Lifters Miss This Triceps Exercise Difference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dLXEdFwhS8E)

> Clear, biomechanically sound explanation of a subtle but meaningful difference between similar-looking exercises, with practical problem-solving applications for pain and performance.

**Key points**
- Two cable triceps extension variations load the shoulder joint in opposite directions depending on body position relative to the cable.
- First variation (cable inside shoulder joint): pulls arm into adduction with minimal shoulder load; mostly a stretch-focused triceps extension with the elbow drifting forward only near lockout.
- Second variation (cable outside shoulder joint): pushes upper arm backward like a pressing motion; loads the shoulder differently and biases the lateral and medial triceps heads over the long head.
- The direction of force relative to joint position matters for muscle recruitment patterns, shoulder stability demands, and pain/discomfort outcomes.
- Practical application: if one variation causes shoulder or elbow discomfort, switching the cable position may redistribute forces to involve different stabilizers or muscle groups.
- The video teaches a general framework for analyzing exercise biomechanics (identifying loaded joints, force direction relative to joints) applicable beyond this single example.

**Takeaway:** Small postural shifts that change where the cable loads the shoulder joint transform a triceps extension from a stretch-focused isolation movement into a press-like exercise, altering shoulder torque direction, muscle recruitment, and comfort — teaching a replicable framework for exercise analysis.

**🔍 Reality check:** The biomechanical reasoning is sound and grounded in joint loading principles, but individual responses to force direction vary; shoulder pain has many causes (technique, mobility, underlying pathology, fatigue), and switching variations alone won't address all of them. The video does not claim to diagnose or treat pain, only to offer one tool for adjustment—this is appropriate. No major overclaiming detected."
