## [The U.K's New Wealth Tax](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/pNsKE9xLAYg)

**Chris Williamson** · 🟡 Skim
> The clip summarizes two standard positions (revenue opportunity vs. competitiveness risk) without new evidence, data, or resolution; the full episode may offer more depth.

**Key points**
- Gary Stevenson proposes a 2% wealth tax on assets over £10 million in the UK, estimated to raise £25 billion annually
- A counterargument presented: GDP could fall 4-5% as foreign investors relocate to lower-tax countries like France, Sweden, or the US
- The underlying tension: wealth redistribution via taxation versus competitive disadvantage in attracting high-net-worth individuals and investment
- Williamson frames the issue as countries needing to compete for capital in a globalized economy where mobility is high

**Takeaway:** A wealth tax could address inequality but risks capital flight if the UK doesn't remain competitively attractive compared to other nations.

**🔍 Reality check:** Both the £25 billion revenue figure and the 4-5% GDP decline are presented as claims without supporting detail, modeling, or source citation. The comparison to other countries' tax rates and actual migration patterns is asserted but not substantiated. Wealth taxes are genuinely complex (France's was largely ineffective before repeal; Switzerland retains them with lower rates), and real-world outcomes depend heavily on design, enforcement, and global coordination—none of which is explored here. This is opinion-based political economy, not settled fact."
