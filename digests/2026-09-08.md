## [Why Gold Crashes During Every War](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/3geoNqDKLQg)

**Felix & Friends (Goat Academy)** · 🟡 Skim
> The core mechanism is clearly explained but the 60-second format offers limited depth, evidence, or historical examples to validate the "every time" claim.

**Key points**
- War → oil prices rise → inflation spikes
- Higher inflation → higher US government bond yields
- Institutional investors move from gold/silver (no yield) to bonds (5%+ yields)
- This pattern repeats across multiple conflicts
- Initial metals decline occurs during wartime inflation spikes

**Takeaway:** Wars typically trigger initial declines in gold and silver prices because rising oil costs fuel inflation, prompting interest rate increases that make yield-bearing US government bonds more attractive than non-yielding precious metals.

**🔍 Reality check:** The video presents a causal chain as near-certain ("every time") without citing specific historical precedent or acknowledging exceptions or lag times. Institutional portfolio flows during wartime are complex and depend on duration of conflict, geopolitical impact, and Fed policy—not a mechanical rule. The short format prevents discussing nuance like flight-to-safety rallies in gold during some crises."

## [Is resilience innate or learnable?](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/2tjFHDu-wJI)

**Vinh Giang** · 🟡 Skim
> The core insight is clear and useful (resilience is built through voluntary struggle), but the video is short and anecdotal with no research, data, or depth.

**Key points**
- Resilience is coachable and learnable, not a fixed trait.
- Overprotecting children from difficulty backfires—removed challenges led to increased fear in author's son.
- Exposure to manageable struggle (falling, failing) builds resilience and 'thick skin'—contrasted with his two-year-old daughter who doesn't cry after falls.
- Choosing to do hard things (e.g., writing a book) is how adults develop resilience and focus.
- Parents must intentionally allow children to struggle and fail to build resilience, rather than removing all difficulty.

**Takeaway:** Resilience is learnable, not innate; it develops through doing difficult things and being allowed to struggle, not by having challenges removed for you.

**🔍 Reality check:** This is opinion based on personal parenting experience, not validated research. The causal claim that removing difficulty caused his son's fearfulness, and that his daughter's fearlessness comes from exposure, rests on two anecdotes and ignores temperament, age differences, and other variables. The idea that resilience is "learnable" is popular but still debated in psychology—twin studies suggest heritable components exist. No acknowledgment of the balance between appropriate support and productive struggle, or of how trauma differs from manageable challenge.

## [HOW TO DEAL WITH TOXIC PEOPLE](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/1KTmrgR2VYg)

**The Diary Of A CEO** · ✅ Watch in full
> Practical, evidence-informed tactics applicable to workplace and personal relationships; moves beyond vague advice to specific behavioral interventions grounded in personality psychology.

**Key points**
- Establish explicit rules and clear boundaries to reduce selfish behavior—ambiguity enables it
- Use rewards (status, bonuses, recognition) rather than punishment; high-psychopathy brains don't process punishment effectively
- Find win-win negotiations by identifying what actually motivates them, which may differ from standard incentives
- Emphasize shared identity or common ground when giving feedback; narcissists respond better when they see you as part of their in-group
- Detect manipulation by looking for lack of verifiable details, vague language, and signs of cognitive strain—not just eye contact or fidgeting
- Use text-based communication over face-to-face when possible to neutralize their charisma advantage

**Takeaway:** When dealing with people displaying dark personality traits (narcissism, psychopathy), you can't change them, but you can manage interactions through clear boundaries, understanding their motivations, and using specific behavioral strategies rather than punishment.

**🔍 Reality check:** The video assumes you want to keep or work with these people; firing/distancing is sometimes the better option and isn't explored. The framing treats "dark personality traits" as a stable diagnosis, but traits exist on spectra and clinical diagnosis requires professional assessment. The deception-detection cues mentioned (lack of detail, cognitive strain) have modest predictive value in research and are context-dependent; not foolproof. No mention of personal cost or emotional toll of managing such relationships, which may outweigh benefits. This is descriptive psychology, not a guarantee these strategies will work with every individual.

## [Local AI Is Better Than You Think (Gemma, HuggingFace etc)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UtFo1ZNC2ns)

**Greg Isenberg** · ✅ Watch in full
> Comprehensive, actionable primer for non-technical founders covering vocabulary, tools, three working prototypes, and three concrete business ideas with customer segments and first steps clearly mapped out.

**Key points**
- Local AI means models run on your hardware (laptop, phone, device); cloud AI runs elsewhere. The key question is 'good enough for the job?' not 'smarter than the largest model.'
- Four pieces: the model (Gemma, Llama, Mistral), the warehouse (HuggingFace), the software (LM Studio, Ollama), and the workflow you build. Start by reading model cards for purpose, size, license, hardware, inputs, and quantized versions.
- Gemma 4 comes in sizes from E2B (phones) to 26B/31B (workstations). Specialized variants include EmbeddingGemma (search), FunctionGemma (tool use), PaliGemma (vision), ShieldGemma (safety).
- Three paths to run models: LM Studio (desktop app, user-friendly), Ollama (command-line, developer-oriented with local API), Google AI Edge/LiteRT-LM (for shipping on-device apps).
- Hybrid architecture wins: local handles private/sensitive data first, cloud handles heavy reasoning, humans approve important work. Start with one repeated workflow (one folder, one model, one output) and run it 10 times before fine-tuning.
- Three startup ideas: home health QA reviewer (flag documentation issues before billing/audit), offline field report co-pilot for restoration (photo+notes→draft report on-site), presend reviewer for professional services (flag risky language in emails/contracts before sending).

**Takeaway:** Local AI is viable for practical business workflows—not because it's smarter, but because it keeps sensitive data private, runs offline, and reduces latency. The 24-month opportunity window favors founders who identify repetitive workflows and pair local models with hybrid cloud architecture, rather than waiting for perfect models.
